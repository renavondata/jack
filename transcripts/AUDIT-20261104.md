# Audit: *A Court for Agents* against jack, and both against their principles

*An outside read, 4–5 November 2026, on `gfrmin/agent-court` at `de03049` (4 Nov 13:02Z) and `renavondata/jack` at `451d13c` (`main`, 4 Nov 15:37Z; log to seq 1336, 2026-11-04T15:28:28Z). Nothing here is on either record; every claim names the file, seq or tool run it rests on. Findings are ordered by what they cost if left.*

## 0. Verdict

- **The book and jack mostly reflect each other, and where they do not it is the book that is wrong** — in six places that matter (§1). The worst are two: the book cites a charter clause as a working mechanism that was never built (A2.8, the monitor the owner does not operate), and it attributes the liveness null p₀ to assize's series when the brief says it came from jack's own first nine items.
- **The book is logically well built, with one foundational gap and two self-contradictions** (§2). The gap: chapter 0 founds "optimal" on expected utility and appendix A claims Wald's optimality for the court's decision procedure, but the court decides by anytime-valid tests at α = 0.05 and by regret bounds; no loss, no Bayes rule, and the "stop" that §12.2 files as a theorem (E ≥ 20) hides a convention (α) with no provenance. The contradictions: "two acts and a wait" (ch 9) vs "three" (ch 4); the cap moved "five times" (chs 4, 8) vs "four" (ch 16) vs three per SPEC §10.
- **jack follows its principles where they are code and lapses where they are prose.** The kernel and judge do what SPEC says, the tests and 115 pins pass, the pushes resolve to the commits the book names (§3.1). Three code defects against the texts (§3.6): the two checkers disagree on a reachable line (`NaN`), which nothing has generated; the kernel's 450/500-line bound is enforced only at `promote`; and the clerk's W1 writ class exists in code for C1b only, so the window now running has no forecast writs. Of the charter's four §12 falsifiers one holds, one has arguably fired and two cannot fire as written; and the two statistics the book presents as evidence — the liveness wealth and the procedure:object ratio — are now driven by the court's own cron stream, which is the Goodhart case chapter 2 §2.5 names (§3.3).
- **meta-jack is where the method is thinnest.** Every real failure since 25 October has been in the layer the briefs and rulings govern (windows, decision kinds, the stated end, attribution), none in the kernel; that layer is Python with hard-coded constants and English with two meanings per term, changed by rulings that are not entries, and it has produced a court that cannot open its next window before 17 November because of an ambiguity its own texts created (§4).
- **Formalising jack's logic is possible, cheap, and worth doing for exactly that layer** — not the kernel. One typed model (state, step, the judge's grounds, a `Window` with one `end`, three-valued criteria, a sum type for decision kinds) would have refused three of the four texts that caused the rulings of 4 November (§5).

## 2. The book's logic

**2.1 The foundation and the mechanism disagree.** Ch 0 §0.1: "optimally" means maximise expected utility, "a theorem three times over" (Cox, Savage, Wald). App A §A.17: the court's decision procedure is Wald-optimal because "every discretionary choice… is made as a bet with a stated probability and scored, so the court's decision procedure is one that cannot be dominated within the class it competes in." That does not follow. Being scored makes a procedure *evaluable*; it does not make it a Bayes rule. The court's actual decisions are: promote iff every criterion holds and E ≥ 20 (a test at level α); invite by EXP3 (a regret bound); route by counsel's call. None is derived from a loss function; no loss function is stated anywhere in CHARTER, SPEC or a brief (contrast Guy's life-agent OQ-0, where u_wrong is the first question). The honest statement — the book's §12.2 already half makes it for the stake — is that the court is a game-theoretic/frequentist construction (Shafer–Vovk, regret) chosen *because* the owner's utility is unknown, and that EU appears only as the standard the owner's wishes are not graded against. Either drop "Wald" from §A.17 and the "three theorems" from §0.1, or supply the loss and show one decision is Bayes under it. Cox's theorem also needs its hypotheses stated (Halpern 1999 is the standard caveat) if §4's register ("a result is stated with its hypotheses") is to apply to chapter 0.

**2.2 A constant with no provenance sits inside the "theorem" row.** §12.2's table: "the level — E ≥ 20 — 1/α at α = 0.05; Ville's inequality". Ville gives P(sup E ≥ 1/α) ≤ α for *any* α; 0.05 is a convention, and counsel's own COUNSEL-OPEN row 28 calls "the 20s, p₀" conventions dressed as rules. By §12.10's test ("a constant with no provenance… the chapter has an authority in it and must say which") the chapter has one and should say so — then the table has two rows that end in neither a bound nor an election (α and the stake), not one.

**2.3 The liveness statistic is self-manufactured, and the ratio guard counts it as object.** Ch 2 §2.5 states the third condition: "its own statistics… can be manufactured by producing more court, so it must count its procedure against its object and stop when the ratio turns." On the record: items are "a herald act landing a timely resolution"; since 1 Nov the resolutions are of hourly `world.renavon` forecasts that a cron template under the reviewer key enters (324 of 342); continued liveness E stands at ≈1.5 × 10²² over 81 items (`status.py --subject C2a --as-of 2026-11-04T15:28:28Z`). The ratio guard reads these as *object* (type `world.*`), so the C2a ratio 320 : 649 "met" is the court scheduling its own objects. Both statistics now measure whether a cron job keeps up with itself. That is operationally useful (it caught the 2–3 Nov stall) but it is not evidence of the kind chs 6, 11 and 13 claim, and the book's C1b figures (67 : 82; E ≈ 25.5 on nine items) are the beginning of the same stream. The honest framing: liveness measures the pipeline's own cadence against a null taken from its first week; the guard measures cadence too. A guard that could not be moved by scheduling would need "object" to mean an entry the court did not itself schedule — which is not decidable from the log (§5).

**2.4 Chapter 6's "which is what it is for" is spin.** §6.4: C2a was refused on criterion 7 (nine repairs under the owner's login), eight of which were "the court's machinery acting under his login, which the acts classifier can only type as his"; the book: "the part of the law that measures the owner's attention refused them anyway, which is what it is for." A criterion that fails on a known misattribution is a coarse instrument producing a false positive, and Guy's decision to let it fail and fix identity first is the right response to a defect — not the law working. Ch 12 §12.4's "He is priced: every act of his is an entry" is likewise aspirational: the pricing runs on a GitHub-login classifier, and this is the first thing a referee will push on.

**2.5 Internal inconsistencies.** (a) "three acts and a wait" (ch 4 §4.3) vs "two acts and a wait" (ch 9 §9.3, CHARTER A2.7). (b) Cap moved five/five/four times (chs 4, 8, 16). (c) Ch 14 §14.8 names proplang's failure as "every incident minted a clause and none retired"; chapters 9 and 12 present jack's five amendments in seventeen days, COUNSEL-OPEN's 35 rows of counsel's self-imposed rules, briefs amended inside windows (BRIEF-C1b 1–2, BRIEF-C2a 1–3), and a ruling with five corrections, without asking whether the same thing is happening one level up. §4.3 below says it is. (d) Ch 15 §15.13's sentence "as counsel's early forecasts were in the other direction on its own harness did" is broken.

**2.6 The checking claim overstates what the checker does.** Front matter: "every figure is recomputed by script on a clone of jack before a chapter is committed, and a chapter that prints a number the script cannot reproduce does not build." `data/facts.toml` registers 20 figures and two self-counts; the chapters print well over a hundred (every seq, ratio, wealth, count). D45 admits the gap ("What remains unchecked is a figure in the prose that nobody has registered"). Say "every registered figure", and register the ones the argument rests on (25.52; 127 : 32; 67 : 82; 386.009170; 605.7; 2.450887; 0.593/0.685; the 83 herald merges are registered, the rest are not).

What the checker does, run here: `tools/check_book.py --jack <clone>` passes at `de03049` against jack's full history — `check_book: ok [facts, counts, citations, quotes, 9 provenance tags rerun] over 21 files`, 4 m 12 s — and `--no-jack` passes. So the registered facts recompute (all 20 execute their `how`: `count_kernel.py`, `status.py` in a worktree at the commit, folds over `log.jsonl`; tampering a value is caught). The gaps, from the code (`tools/check_book.py` line numbers): 20 facts and 30 phrases against ~133 distinct multi-digit figures in the prose; the nine provenance tags are rerun for their *exit status only* (line 517), so a figure printed beside a tag — "about 1.44", "170 : 269", "0.655", "p 0.192" — is never compared with the tag's output; 80 `seq N` references are not checked against the log at all; quotations are checked only when the document tag follows the quote within 60 characters (lines 457–460), so 21 quotations tagged *before* the quote (`[CHARTER §3: "…"]`, five in appendix A alone) and every quotation of a ruling, the log, README, `kernel2/NOTES.md` or an assize document are unchecked — 61 of 242 quoted strings are checked; a depth-1 clone crashes the checker with a traceback rather than a message (line 151); and README's "any chapter or essay" is wrong — essays are never read. None of this is hidden (D45 says so), but the front matter and README promise more than line 517 delivers.

**2.7 Arguments that are sound and should stay.** The e-process algebra (ch 6 §6.2, A4.3–4) is correct as stated: λ < 1/p₀, factors positive, conditional expectation ≤ 1 under the null; the bounded-difference bet is a test supermartingale under E[D | past] ≤ 0; the exponentiated score sum is rightly excluded. Rice, Löb/Gödel, Kalai–Lehrer/Nachbar, Alpern–Schneider, Bahadur–Savage, Conant–Ashby and Ashby are stated with their hypotheses and not overstated; §12.1–12.7's residues are the best part of the book. The stake critique in §12.2 is exactly right and should be the model for §2.2's α.

## 4. meta-jack: the process that builds jack

**4.1 The amendment path is the assize process with better bookkeeping.** Guy's own verdict (row 33) is the right one and the book should carry it: an amendment is prose with no test, priced by near-unlosable forecasts (A4's seq 60 at p 0.5 "cited or refuses an act by January"; A5's seq 121 at p 0.65 "in force by 8 Nov", resolved by Guy's own enactment), drafted, read-selected and recommended by one party. Five in seventeen days; the sixth drafted within an hour of a failure its drafter helped cause. The pin discipline that governs code governs none of this.

**4.2 Rulings are the real change mechanism and they are off the log.** Since A3 the law has moved by rulings (25 Oct ledger-first; 4 Nov window-fix; the "no amendment now" decision; the attention direction) recorded as transcripts and COUNSEL-OPEN rows. CASES.md: "Rulings are not entries on the log"; "No text ranks a ruling before it is recorded"; "no text says what displaces a ruling." ## 5. Formalising jack's logic — possible, cheap, and worth it for the layer that keeps failing

**5.1 What is already formal in everything but notation.** `kernel/check.py` is a fold: `State` (ten fields, a namedtuple) × line → `State` or `Refuse(reason)`, pure, no I/O; SPEC §7–8 is its prose. The judge is fourteen predicates over two trees and two logs. The two wealth processes are three-line supermartingale lemmas. Transcribing the kernel into a typed model (Lean 4 structures with decidable propositions, or Alloy/TLA+ for model checking — the language matters less than one field per concept) is a day's work and about the size of SPEC §1–8. Properties provable of the *model* (not the Python): determinism; sticky refusal; prospectivity (the verdict at i is a function of entries < i); one blob one id; the last-key invariant (every valid prefix leaves a key holding `key`); `activate_at` > `seq`; `charter_at` monotone. These are the properties the 100,000-seed differential tests by sampling.

**5.2 Why it is not worth doing for the kernel alone.** The kernel has not failed on anything generated. Two implementations, a committed seed list, 636,392 prefixes with no disagreement, and every disagreement found so far by reviews, byte probes or — today — a hand-typed `NaN` (REPORT.md §8.4; §3.6.1), never by a seed. A proof of a model says nothing about `check.py` unless the Python is extracted from it, which would change the trusted base; what the `NaN` case asks for is cheaper than a proof: a generator driven by the JSON grammar's edge tokens (`NaN`, `Infinity`, a BOM, `\r\n`, U+2028) rather than by the attack list alone, because the differential is exactly as strong as its generator.

**5.3 Why it is worth doing for the judge-and-window layer.** Every ruling and every correction since 25 October is a failure of that layer, and each is one a typed object would have refused at the brief:
1. *Two ends for one window.* A5.2: end := `resolves_by` of the promotion forecast, "a brief states no other date". BRIEF-C2a v2: "fourteen days at most" *and* "V0's `resolves_by` is the window's end". A record `Window {opens: Seq; end: Instant; …}` has one `end`; a brief carrying two is ill-typed. Two cold reads passed the brief; the cost is that no window can open before 17 Nov.
2. *One kind, two meanings.* A2.6 uses `decision fix` for the boundary act; BRIEF-C1b uses it for a mid-window repair; `status.py` implemented the second. `DecisionKind = Veto | Promote | Budget | Grant | Transmute | FixWindow(window) | Repair(what)` makes the collision a compile error, and R2 of the ruling is exactly that type.
3. *Unknown read as false.* Correction 2 lists four criteria `status.py` prints as unmet when the evidence is merely absent. `Criterion = Met | Unmet | Unknown(missing)` in the spec; the tool collapses the third into the second.
4. *Two doctrines of amendment order.* A2.7 "the law changes, then the code" and RUNBOOK "amend last, so the charter never says what the code does not enforce" are two invariants — I1: no `push` in (`amend.seq`, `activate_at`); I2: at every `main` commit the charter's text ⊆ what kernel + judge enforce. I1 holds; I2 does not (A2.8, §3's witnesses and lapse, §5's ladder) and no text claims it. Writing both down would end the book's and the runbook's disagreement in one line.
5. *The ratio guard.* "object := forecast type starts with `world.`" is decidable and measures cadence. The intended predicate — "an outcome process reads no entry of the record *and* the court did not schedule the entry" — is not decidable from the log. Formalising forces the charter to choose: keep the decidable guard and stop calling it a Goodhart guard, or add a field (`scheduled_by`) that makes the second predicate decidable.
6. *The null.* p₀ = 6/11 is Laplace over nine jack items under a superseded attribution; the window's Y_i is a different variable from assize's "items without repair". Writing the null as a statement about Y as defined makes the "versus the predecessor" reading disappear, which is correct.

**5.4 What stays English, correctly.** Merit; what a claim means (world types are English contracts with a hash in the middle); the owner's utility; what the charter allows (rulings). Chapter 8 §8.3 already says this and is right. The exercise is to shrink the English to those four.

**5.5 What it is not.** Not the stratified-Datalog law language of CHARTER §3 and A2.1, which waits for a conduct case recognition cannot refuse. The point here is narrower: the de facto law language *already exists* — it is `tools/status.py`, with `P0 = Fraction(6, 11)`, caps 7,800 and 6,900, and a function per window name hard-coded, so that every new window is a code change that lands inside the window it governs (BRIEF-C2a-prime, correction 3). The choice is not Datalog-or-nothing; it is Python-with-constants or a declared, data-form window spec that `status.py` reads the way `kernel2` reads SPEC.

**5.6 The smallest exercise** (one builder session, another family, from the texts; a C2c obligation if Guy wants it on the record): (a) `spec/model.*` — `State`, entry variants with exact fields, `step`, the fourteen grounds as predicates, `Window`, three-valued `Criterion`, `DecisionKind`; (b) check the six invariants above; (c) diff the model's vocabulary against SPEC and every brief: a term in a brief with no field in the model is a finding ("inside the window", "stated end", "repair", "object", "late report", "finalised item" are the first six).
