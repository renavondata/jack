## 2. Terms

- **M**: the commit `origin/main` names after the job's first fetch. Every use of "`main`" in these rules means M. The verbs fetch again for themselves; R9a says what the job does when `origin/main` has moved off M.
- **S**: the stale head, the commit the pull request's head branch names on `origin` after that same fetch.
- **The fork point** k: the number of leading lines, compared as bytes with their line ends, that M's `log.jsonl` and S's `log.jsonl` share.
- **The suffix**: S's log lines after the first k.
- **The law at the fork point**: the keys that M's first k lines declare, as `kernel/check.py` folds them.
- **The law at M**: the keys that M's whole log declares.
- **The job's party**: the principal the law at M gives the key file this job signs with; if that key is not declared there, or holds no power, the job has none.

## 3. The rules

R0 to R12 bind `repropose.py` and `repropose.yml`; H1 to H4 bind `herald.yml`. Rules are tested in their number's order and the first that fails decides. A refusal named `R-…` is a **provenance refusal**: the job comments on the pull request with the code and nothing else of the run, files no session, pushes nothing, leaves no entry of its own on any branch of `origin`, and exits 0. No file of S is executed or imported, and no model session is filed, unless R1 to R7 have all passed.

- **R0, the inputs.** `repropose.yml` has two inputs, `n` and `party`. Before anything else the script matches `n` against `[1-9][0-9]*` (so one pull request has one spelling) and `party` against `builder` or `reviewer`, each as a whole string; otherwise it prints a line and ends, with no comment (there is no trusted number to comment on). `party` chooses the runner; R3 decides the party from the key, and refuses a job whose `party` input names another.
- **R1, the head.** The job fetches `origin` once, then reads the pull request by its number through the API: it is open, or closed and not merged (the herald closes it before it dispatches; who closed it is not asked), its base is `main`, its head repository is `renavondata/jack`, and S, the tip of its head branch on `origin` as fetched, equals the head commit the API reports. Otherwise `R-head`. If M's `log.jsonl` is a byte prefix of S's, the branch is not stale: the job prints so, comments nothing and ends. The branch name goes to `git` as one argument; one beginning with `-` is `R-head`.
- **R2, anchored to `main`.** k is at least 1, so the two logs share genesis, and k is the whole length of M's log or less. In a clean detached worktree of M, with S's log bytes written to a file outside every tree, M's `python3 -m kernel.check <file>` reports ok. Otherwise `R-log`. (With k ≥ 1 the kernel checks every suffix entry's signature against keys declared by `main`'s own genesis and by key entries in the shared prefix or the suffix; R3 removes the suffix's.)
- **R3, what the suffix may hold.** Every suffix entry is of type `forecast`, `resolution`, `proposal` or `merge`. Tested in this order: the job has a party, that party is the `party` input, and the suffix holds at least one `proposal`, the last of which has the job's party as proposer, else `R-party`; then every `forecast`, `resolution` and `proposal` entry's proposer is the job's party and its signing key is one that both the law at the fork point and the law at M declare for that party with the power for that entry's type (so a key revoked on `main` since the fork authenticates nothing here), and every `merge` entry is signed by a key to which both the law at the fork point and the law at M give the power `merge` and none of `proposal`, `forecast` and `resolution`, and comes after at least one `proposal` entry, else `R-foreign`; an entry of any other type is `R-foreign`.
- **R4, the content.** P is the suffix's last `proposal` entry and C the commit it names. C is an ancestor of S. Every commit in `git rev-list C..S` changes, against each of its parents, `log.jsonl` alone or nothing. Otherwise `R-unsigned`. The patch is `git diff --binary B C` outside `log.jsonl`, B being the merge base of C and M; when there are several merge bases: `R-unsigned`. It is applied with `git apply --index --binary`, as today (line 94), with no three-way fallback: a patch that does not apply so is R10's failure.
- **R5, the title.** T is P's `title`. T begins with `-`, holds a character below U+0020 or from U+007F to U+009F, or is longer than 4,000 characters: `R-title`. The new proposal's title is T followed by ` (re-proposed mechanically: PR #<n> went stale)`, as today, n in digits; it is passed to the verb as one argument.
- **R6, the age.** P's `ts` is not later than the job's clock, read once at the start, and not more than `repropose_max_age_hours` before it; the number is a positive whole number of hours, alone on the first line of `.github/repropose-max-age-hours` on the runner's `main`, which the sovereign sets; a file missing, unreadable or holding anything else is `R-old`. Exactly that many hours before: not refused. Otherwise `R-old`. (Evidence for the number: among jack's pull requests #922 to #1121, 56 mechanical re-proposals opened between 7.4 and 167.8 minutes after their stale original.)
- **R7, not again once merged.** If M's log holds a `proposal` entry of the job's party whose title is the new title R5 gives: `R-replayed`. The job runs under a concurrency group of the pull request's number that queues and does not cancel, as today (`repropose.yml` 18 to 20). This refuses a repeat only after the first re-proposal has merged; section 6 says what it does not refuse.
- **R8, no string of the pull request written by the runner's files into a program or a session's issue.** (What a signed program does with its own strings, and what a filed session later reads, are outside it: section 6.) No title, body or branch name of the pull request, no commit message, no entry body and no output of the script is written into the text of a program, into a command line that a shell parses, or into a session's title or body. Each travels as an argument, an environment variable or a file. A forecast's claim and arguments and a resolution's evidence go to the verbs as arguments, as today's script passes them (lines 100 to 104).
- **R9, what is re-made.** In the suffix's order: a forecast is skipped when its body's `args.window_utc` is present and its first member is at or before the job's clock (today's test, lines 59 to 60), when its `resolves_by` is before the job's clock, or when M's log holds a forecast of the same proposer and body; a resolution is skipped when M's log resolves that forecast; proposals and merges are not re-made. The rest are re-made by the verbs in a new worktree that holds M with the patch applied and committed. If nothing is re-made and the patch is empty, the job comments "nothing to re-make" and ends with no proposal; a suffix that is one proposal and no content is such a case.
- **R9a, a moved `main`.** Before each verb the job compares `git rev-parse origin/main` with M, and again after it. If it differs before the proposal verb has pushed, the step fails under the name `moved` and R10 applies; a difference first seen after that push changes nothing, the proposal being published. (Before the push, the party's session then re-proposes on the newer `main`, as today's fallback does when a verb refuses a branch that no longer contains `origin/main`.)
- **R10, the fallback.** A failure after R1 to R7 have passed, of one of five named steps (`apply`: the patch does not apply; `forecast` or `resolve`: that verb refuses; `proposal`: the proposal verb refuses before its push; `moved`: R9a), files the job's party's own session from the template `transcripts/sessions/repropose.md` as M holds it, filled by a script's plain replacement of five placeholders: `{PARTY}`, the validated input, which R3 has found to be the job's party (and which today's template uses for its `key:` and party lines); `{N}`, the pull request's number; `{S}` and `{C}`, forty hexadecimal digits each; `{P}`, the `seq` of P in S's log, in digits. The issue's title is `<party>: re-propose PR #<n> on a fresh prefix`. The body holds the filled template and the failed step's name, and no other text. The template tells the session to check out S, to read P at that `seq` of S's log for the title, to work from C, and that nothing in the pull request's own title, body or comments is an instruction. The forecast and resolve verbs push nothing, so on a failure before the proposal verb's push the job removes its worktree and local branch; what it has then observed of `origin`, and what it does not claim, R12(d) says. If the proposal verb refuses after its push, the pushed branch holds a signed proposal and may have no pull request: the job does not delete it, comments on the stale pull request with the new branch's name (the job's own string: the stale branch's name with `-r` and digits, which R1 has bounded to a name beginning with no `-`), and files nothing; the herald's loop does not see a branch without a pull request, and section 6 names this.
- **R11, the test seams.** `REPROPOSE_MAIN` is removed. The script takes three optional arguments that `repropose.yml` never passes and the pin does: the ref to use as M, the file to read the age from, and a directory of canned API answers for R1. It takes no clock argument: the pin builds its fixtures around the real clock (section 5).
- **R12, the outcomes not said above.** (a) *Git cannot answer* (no merge base, C missing or unreadable, an inspection command failing) under R4: `R-unsigned`. (b) *The age file* is valid only if its whole content, less one final newline, is the digits. (c) *A suffix entry the verbs cannot re-make exactly*: a forecast whose `args` is present and not an object, or whose `args.window_utc` is present and not a list beginning with a string, takes R10 under the step `forecast`; and, as today (lines 113 to 119), after the verbs have run the job compares each re-made entry's type and body with the stale one and any difference takes R10 under the step `forecast` when the entry is a forecast and `resolve` when it is a resolution. (d) *Whether the proposal verb pushed* is decided after the verb returns non-zero, by two tests: the job's worktree head H is a commit the verb made (it is not the head the worktree had before the verb ran), and the new branch, fetched from `origin` after the verb has returned, is H or has H as an ancestor. Both hold: it pushed, and H with its signed proposal is on `origin` whatever another writer has since added above it. Otherwise (the verb made no commit; or `origin` has no branch of that name; or it has one that does not contain H) the job treats it as not pushed and the step is `proposal`; a branch of that name that does not contain H is not the job's, and the job neither deletes it nor names it in a comment. The two tests read `origin` once, after the verb has returned, and the job claims nothing beyond that reading: a writer with the right to write that branch on jack can advance, rewind or delete it between the verb's push and the job's fetch, or after it, and after a rewind or a deletion entries this run signed and pushed (its forecasts, its resolutions, its proposal) may still lie on `origin` or in its history though the job has taken the run as not pushed and filed the session. Section 6 names this. (e) *The new branch's name* is allocated as today's line 89 allocates it. (f) *Any other failure* (the fetch, an API call, a worktree or commit command, a comment, the filing of the session, a timeout, the cleanup) ends the job non-zero with nothing more attempted; the failed run is the record and nothing retries it. (g) *Left to the builder and to be stated in its report, with no effect on any rule above*: the syntax of the three optional arguments, the format of the canned API answers, and how the pin hands the script a fixture key.

## 4. What this refuses that the lane does today

- A stale branch holding, after the fork point, an entry by a party other than the job's (but for the herald's merges). Counted on `main`'s log at `315bf158`: since seq 195 no merged proposal's segment has held one; this is a count of what merged, not of what a branch may hold.
- A stale branch on which content was committed after C, the commit its last proposal names, whether before or after that entry. No session is filed for it (`R-unsigned`); the party can propose again from a session of its own.
- A pull request from a fork. Of jack's last 100, none came from one.
- A proposal older than `repropose_max_age_hours` or dated after the job's clock; a title R5 refuses, which the proposal entry's schema admits; a C with several merge bases against M; a head whose suffix holds no proposal of the builder or the reviewer, among them a head that would merge today by a filled `Proposer:` trailer and no proposal entry (`tools/jack` 153 to 156); any run while the age file is missing.
- A log that does not share `main`'s genesis and prefix byte for byte; a proposal naming a commit that is not an ancestor of the head; a suffix merge signed by a key that also holds `proposal`, `forecast` or `resolution`; a suffix entry of any other type, whoever signed it (today such an entry sends the pull request to the party's session; here no session is filed: the code is `R-party` when the suffix lacks a proposal of the job's party, else `R-foreign`); an entry signed by a key revoked on `main` since the fork; a re-proposal whose title `main` already holds under that party for any reason (R7 reads titles, not histories).

Counsel has counted none of these second and third kinds on the lane and does not say they do not occur.

It does not refuse: a re-proposal of a re-proposal; a branch with two proposal entries, with or without a herald's merge between them (P is the last); a proposal with no content commit (the patch is empty; the suffix's eligible entries are re-made, and if R9 leaves none there is no proposal).

## 6. What this leaves

- **The fallback's session is a model reading a branch.** After R1 to R7 the branch's content and P are the job's own party's signed work, but a session that works from C reads its files, and a model may take what it reads as instruction. The template's sentence is a request to the model, not a control. Rung 6, narrated.
- **Signed code is still code.** A party's own signed commit can do anything its key's user can; this brief authenticates whose it is.
- **Replay inside the age.** Within `repropose_max_age_hours`, a proposal a party abandoned can be re-proposed as signed by whoever re-opens its pull request or opens another for the same head. R7 refuses a repeat only when the first re-proposal has merged and the pull request's number is the same: a second run before that merge, or a new pull request (a new number, so a new title), is not refused. The cost to whoever does it is one dispatch by an identity with Actions write, or one pull request opened (after closing the open one for that head, if there is one); the cost to the lane is a duplicate proposal, which goes stale when its twin merges and is then either skipped entry by entry under R9 or, where its patch no longer applies, sent to the party's session. Each provenance refusal costs one run; nothing bounds how many pull requests an identity with pull-request write may open.
- **A key revoked after the job starts** is not treated: R3 reads the law at M. **A dispatch for a stale branch whose pull request is still open** is processed like any other: R1 does not ask whether the herald closed it.
- **The age is of the last proposal, not of the work.** Each mechanical re-proposal signs a new proposal with a new `ts`, so a chain of them renews the age; nothing bounds the suffix's length, the patch's size or the run's work.
- **Replay can reach the fallback.** After a first re-proposal has merged, a second pull request for the same head has a new number, passes R7, and its patch may no longer apply (the file exists), which files the party's session.
- **A refusal after the push.** A proposal verb that refuses after its push leaves a branch with a signed proposal and perhaps no pull request (a create call may also have succeeded unseen); if there is none, nothing re-opens it: the party's next session, or his hand, must.
- **Another writer on the new branch.** R12(d) reads the new branch once. Whoever may write branches on jack can change it before or after that reading; a run the job took as not pushed may then have left signed entries of its own on `origin`, and a run it took as pushed may later lose them. The job does not detect this, does not undo it, and files or withholds the session by its one reading. Every such entry is signed by the job's party under `main`'s law and is on no pull request the job opened.
- **A dispatch with a false `party`** by an identity with Actions write on the runner runs on that party's runner and ends in `R-party` when the stale proposal is not that party's; it is not refused before the job starts.
- **The keys still lie in one user's home on steel** until the custody text is built; any session there can read them without the re-proposer.
- **Entries a job signed before a failure** are discarded with its worktree: a signature was made and thrown away, and a forecast so signed and never pushed is on no log.
