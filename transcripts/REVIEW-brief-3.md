## 2. What in the scope is unnecessary to reach C1a's exit?

- **An extensible resolver convention (`type` plus arbitrary `args`).** A defined `type` is needed for criterion 4 and the scoring groups. General resolver arguments and mechanical-resolver compatibility are not needed to resolve ten forecasts by hand; the ruling explicitly chooses manual resolution before the clerk.
- **Forecast power for every party, resolution power for all three model roles, and a separate master main-verb route.** Three forecasting principals and enough independent resolution authority suffice for the numerical exit. Broad participation and the master route are charter-compatible conveniences, not necessary evidence for the stated threshold.
- **Overdue-list reporting and the completed C1b brief.** They prepare the next window; no C1a criterion bounds overdue forecasts or demands a clerk. J7 and the ruling's “what would show (a) wrong” must not silently become exit conditions.
- **Stopping the entire layer for an unrepresentable requested forecast.** The layer can complete its binary test without accepting every desired forecast representation.

The pins, simulator extension, 10⁵ differential, 500-line bound and copied-ledger witness are necessary to the literal exit because its criteria expressly require them. A2.4 authorises those local tests. The amendment, real signatures and independent replay are not optional merely because counts could be manufactured without them.

## 5. Can two independent checkers implement the SPEC patch from its text alone?

**Not yet as a complete, charter-consistent specification.** The new forecast/resolution transition is substantially clearer than the surrounding integration. Given SPEC plus the patch, two authors can independently implement: body shapes; deadline comparison; prior-forecast lookup; single resolution; authentication before self-judgement; and state updates only on acceptance. It is explicit that missing/already-resolved references fail at step 7, before signatures, and self-judgement fails only after all signature checks. Thus a self-resolution with a bad signature is `bad_signature`, not `self_judged`; an already-resolved self-reference is `schema`. Those cases are not unresolved ambiguities.

The remaining ambiguities and conflicts are:

1. **Reason vocabulary across checker and harness.** §9 becomes thirteen; §9a.3 remains twelve. One author can follow the enumerated §9 set, another the unchanged literal harness requirement. Even agreeing checkers may then fail the run of record. The patch must explicitly update every normative code-set reference.
2. **Timestamp law and failure precedence.** A2.2 requires `ts_decreased`; SPEC §4 and §12.4 retain C0's no-ordering rule, and the patch adds only the within-forecast `resolves_by >= ts` test. A checker written only to patched SPEC accepts decreasing timestamps; a charter-following checker must reject them. No insertion point says whether `ts_decreased` precedes `schema`, `bad_signature` or `self_judged`. Adding it also makes fourteen codes, contradicting A3.3's “thirteen”. This is an inherited conflict left unresolved, not a defect in lexical comparison of two valid UTC instants.
3. **Activation versus installation.** The patch supplies no type-availability or new-rule activation rule. Its natural reading enables the new vocabulary and self-resolution constraint everywhere when the binary changes; A3 speaks of force from `activate_at`, after the installing push. State explicitly whether recognition is intentionally installed in advance, what is lawful in the interval, and whether any height-gating is required. Independent authors must not invent that choice or infer an activation height from external files. Earlier valid logs remain replayable under the additive schemas, but that fact alone does not settle legal availability in the interval.
4. **Integer edge cases should be explicit at the new schema boundary.** SPEC distinguishes JSON booleans and integers and explicitly excludes booleans for existing index fields; the new `p` and `outcome` rows should likewise state that `true` and `false` are not integers. The intended mathematical reading is rejection, not permission to accept a language's boolean-as-integer coercion. Include these boundary vectors rather than leave that cross-language trap to custom. Bounds and integer-only canonical numbers otherwise settle floats and out-of-range probabilities.
5. **Editorial insertion and vector contract.** The §12 addition is numbered 6 although decision 6 already exists; it must append or be renumbered, not replace the weak-key decision. `46_forecast_permille_bad` is said to cover both 0 and 1000, but one refused log stops at its first bad entry; specify two cases or two logs if both boundaries are to be witnessed. The required verdicts should be explicit `(seq, reason)` for the concrete signed fixtures. These are application/test ambiguities, not additional recognition rules.

The following ambiguities concern the outside-kernel score and verbs, **not** the validity predicate. SPEC's instruction that the kernel never reads optional `type`/`args` is clear; a checker must not reject a forecast merely because its type convention is unknown.

- **Type domain and grouping:** missing, null, non-string or unknown `type`; whether `args` has any schema; what makes a type world-facing; whether output is separate marginal groups or proposer × type groups.
- **Score arithmetic:** log base; total versus mean loss; whether mean p covers all or only resolved forecasts; treatment of empty groups; and displayed precision. Strict properness does not select one logarithm base or reporting convention.
- **Base-rate timing:** retrospective empirical frequency scored against the same outcomes, or a prequential predictor using only earlier resolutions; update order and ties; pooled versus per-proposer type history; smoothing/clipping at rates 0 and 1. Without smoothing a prequential rate of 1 followed by a failure gives infinite loss. The forecast's 1–999 bound does not bound this comparator. “½ before any resolution” does not resolve these choices.
- **Filtering:** whether `--since SEQ` selects forecast seqs, resolution seqs or both; whether earlier history remains in the baseline; and how `--by` changes the promised “per proposer and per type” output.
- **Overdue output:** as-of clock, equality at the deadline, and “oldest” by forecast seq, timestamp or deadline. The brief says “from the log alone”, but current overdue status needs an external as-of instant; only scoring can literally use the log alone.
- **Resolution evidence interface:** the synopsis requires `--evidence E` but prose introduces `--file` without its argument/precedence rules, destination naming or hash verification behaviour. It also conflicts with the promised log-only branch commit. A run id, commit id and hash need an unambiguous encoding if tools distinguish them.
- **Verb integration:** “master key” versus a key with a particular principal/power; refusal outside the old eight powers in `jack key`; the one-commit merge precondition; the timestamp used for the forecast verb's “earlier than now” check; and whether the read-only `score` verb is exempt from the common signing/fetch/clean-tree rules. Text should decide these rather than make each builder choose.

The properness pin and a copied assize ledger do not resolve these textual choices retrospectively. A2.9 requires a builder to report the conflicts, not copy another checker or change the acceptance oracle to make a run green.
