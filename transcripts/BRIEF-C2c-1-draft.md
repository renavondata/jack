### R1 Numerals are housed

Everything that reads a window file lives in one module, `tools/window.py` (the builder's): the opening rule, the binding (R9), the kinds (R2), the processes (R10), the writ and ratio lines the file governs. `tools/status.py` keeps the code that closed the earlier windows and dispatches a subject with a file to `window.py`; it is history and R1 does not scan it. R1 scans `tools/window.py` and `tools/value.py` whole: no integer, fraction or float literal outside `{0, 1, −1}`; no string literal that parses as a number or as an instant in §4's `ts` form; except a string that is exactly a key of the window file's `numbers`, `spec_constants` or `history`, used as a lookup into that table. `numbers` holds integers, booleans or integer pairs (a pair is a fraction, numerator first); `spec_constants` the constants the record fixes with the text that fixes each; `history` the closed windows' constants that `value.py` still needs (`c1b_last_seq`, 337), with the text each comes from. A criterion's `args` are of five shapes, declared by its kind: numeric (`counted`, `kernel`, `threshold`, `null`, `grace`, `lead`, `min_rows`, `max` — each a `numbers` key), boolean (`scope_equals_spec`), a path or path prefix (`map`, `shells`, `due_file`), a name or list of names the kind interprets (`shows`, `process`, `proposer`, `type`, `pins`, `rows`, `sources`), or a reference to another top-level member of the file (`frozen`, `inventory` → `undertakings`, `witnesses` → `ratchet`). A process's `null` and `threshold` are numeric. Dates and seqs of windows are found by `opens` and `end`, never written.

**Pin `numerals_housed`.** Twins: (1) `tools/window.py` and `tools/value.py` as proposed pass the scan; (2) every numeric `args` of every criterion, and the `null` and `threshold` of every process, of every window file on `main` resolves to a `numbers` key holding an integer or a pair; (3) `tools/window.py` is the only file under `tools/` that opens a path under `transcripts/windows/` (a scan for the string). ## Amendment 3 (18 November, counsel-5; the verbs that end on `main` follow a moved `main` — before the opening, one clause, a deliverable)

**Why.** Six verbs end by pushing `main`: `key`, `decision`, `amend`, `push`, and the master key's `forecast` and `resolve` (every caller of `main_ending`, `tools/jack` lines 118, 244, 282, 296, 299). Each is refused at its start unless local `main` equals the remote's: "Main verbs require HEAD to equal `origin/main`" (`RUNBOOK.md` line 38; `tools/jack` line 47). Each is refused at its end if its push of `main` fails, as it does when the remote's `main` has moved to a commit that the verb's own commit does not contain: "a push that is not a fast-forward is a refusal, never a retry with force" (`RUNBOOK.md` lines 42–43), and `push()` gives one refusal for every failure of `git push`, whatever its cause (`tools/jack` lines 27–29). The herald merges every few minutes. So the sovereign's pastes were built to wait for a quiet minute, and at C2a-prime's opening one waited about forty-two minutes of his sitting (COUNSEL-OPEN row 68: "because `jack decision` and `jack push` cannot follow a moved `main`"). The record of that day: "Agreed with him the same day: the verbs merge a moved `main` and retry, every check repeated, as a deliverable of C2c-1 with a pin".

**The clause.** The code is the builder's, in `tools/jack`, written to `RUNBOOK.md`'s text for it; that text is counsel's and reaches `main` first, by counsel's proposal, after the sovereign has read this brief ("Text before code", `RUNBOOK.md` lines 183–189). The clause binds the six verbs named above and no other. `merge` is outside it: it signs on the proposal's branch and "has no undo" (`RUNBOOK.md` lines 48–50). A party's branch verbs are outside it.

1. *At the start.* After the fetch, and after the refusals of a tree that is not clean and of a detached `HEAD`, and before the log is read: if `HEAD` equals `origin/main` the verb goes on; if `HEAD` is an ancestor of `origin/main` the verb fast-forwards local `main` to it (`git merge --ff-only`) and goes on; otherwise it refuses, since local `main` then holds a commit the remote lacks. The log's state, the signer's party and key, and everything else the verb reads are read after that, from the tree it then stands on. Today the log is folded and the key identified before the comparison (`tools/jack` lines 35–39, then line 47), so the order changes as well as the refusal.

2. *An attempt.* One attempt is the verb from its first precondition to its push of `main`. Its **base** is the commit `origin/main` named at the attempt's fetch. An attempt fetches once before its push, at its start, so until the push `origin/main` means its base; point 3 is the only later fetch. Every check it makes, every file it reads from its arguments, the instant it signs, the entry it appends and the commit it makes belong to that attempt and to no other. The entry is appended to the log of the tree local `main` then stands on: the base's log for five of the verbs, and for `push` the candidate's (point 6), which may hold entries the base's does not. Its `parent` is the hash of the line before it in that log, as SPEC §3 has it, and is never a commit's hash.

3. *After a push of `main` that fails.* The verb does not conclude from the failure that nothing was pushed. Before it undoes anything it fetches again and compares:
   - (a) the attempt's commit is `origin/main` or an ancestor of it: the push landed. The verb undoes nothing, fast-forwards local `main` to `origin/main`, and ends as a success, printing that the remote accepted a push it had reported as failed;
   - (b) otherwise, `origin/main` is no longer the attempt's base: `main` moved. The verb undoes its effects as it does today (`git reset --hard origin/main`, and its own files under `transcripts/` removed; `tools/jack` lines 79–81) and makes a new attempt (point 4);
   - (c) otherwise the push failed with `main` where it was. The verb undoes its effects and refuses, and it prints what `git push` printed, which today's helper discards (`tools/jack` lines 18–22 and 27–29). It does not try again;
   - (d) the second fetch itself fails: the verb undoes its effects locally and refuses, saying that it could not read the remote and so does not know whether the entry landed, and that the remote's log is to be read before the verb is run again.

   A failure before the push — in the verb's own refusals, its effect, the kernel's refusal of the entry, the commit or `judge/selfcheck.py` — is a refusal as today and never a new attempt. No push is forced, and no attempt pushes anything but a fast-forward of `main`.

4. *A new attempt.* The verb starts again from its first precondition with the arguments it was given. The code it runs and every module it uses are loaded again from the tree it now stands on, and nothing computed, read or signed in an earlier attempt is used. A new process is the plain way to do that; the pin tests what can be observed of it (twins 1 and 8 to 12) and cannot tell a new process from another way that meets those twins. The earlier attempt's entry and signature are on no branch, local or remote. Three things pass from one attempt to the next, as arguments and never through the environment (`RUNBOOK.md` line 36): the number of the attempt, and for `push` the commit BRANCH named at the first attempt and the commit that was the first attempt's base. Each verb's own preconditions are therefore tested again on the new base: `decision promote` fetches the runs for the new `HEAD` and runs the status tool again at a new instant; `amend` tests again that the amendment is transmuted and not yet entered, appends its text to the charter as the new base has it, and computes a default `activate_at` from the new log's length, while an `activate_at` given by hand that the longer log has overtaken is refused by the kernel and ends the verb; `key`, `forecast` and `resolve` are refused wherever the new log refuses them.

5. *The bound.* A verb makes at most five attempts, the first included; `push`, whose every attempt runs the check and may run the differential, at most three. These are two constants of `RUNBOOK.md`'s text, held in `tools/jack` beside its other constants; they are not numbers of a window file, because these verbs run before any window opens and between windows, and `tools/jack` may not open a window file (R1). When the last attempt ends in case (b), the verb undoes its effects, leaves local `main` equal to `origin/main`, and refuses, naming the number: `main moved during each of N attempts`.

6. *`push BRANCH`.* Today the verb refuses "BRANCH not containing `origin/main`" (`RUNBOOK.md` line 110; `tools/jack` line 226), makes its checks in a worktree of BRANCH (line 235), and fast-forwards `main` to BRANCH, "never a merge commit, never a rebase" (`RUNBOOK.md` line 120; `tools/jack` line 239). Under this clause:
   - (a) *BRANCH is a commit.* At the first attempt the verb resolves BRANCH once to a commit, and everything after speaks of that commit and never of the name again. A later attempt refuses if BRANCH no longer names it. The verb never commits to BRANCH and never moves its ref.
   - (b) *The candidate.* If BRANCH's commit contains the base, the candidate is BRANCH's commit, as today, on one new condition: the base's `log.jsonl` must be a byte prefix of the candidate's, and the push is refused if it is not. The record is "an append-only, hash-chained log" (CHARTER §4), and the judge refuses a proposal when "trunk's log is not a byte prefix of the proposal's" (SPEC §10, ground 3); but a push is not judged as a proposal, the verb makes no such comparison (`tools/jack` lines 222–244), and the self-check judges "trunk against itself" (`judge/selfcheck.py`), where the comparison is of a log with itself. So neither the verb nor the self-check compares a pushed branch's log with `main`'s today, and this clause adds the comparison. If BRANCH's commit does not contain the base, the candidate is one merge commit that the verb makes in a temporary worktree. Its first parent is the base and its second BRANCH's commit, so that `main`'s first-parent history stays `main`'s. It is authored and committed under the pusher's git identity, and its message is `Merge main into BRANCH (jack push)` with the pusher's own filled `Proposer:` trailer. Its tree is chosen path by path. Call the single merge base of the two commits the *ancestor*; a path's *value* at a commit is its mode and blob, or its absence. Where BRANCH's value equals the ancestor's, the candidate takes `main`'s value (the base's); where `main`'s value equals the ancestor's, or the two values are equal, it takes BRANCH's; where both differ from the ancestor's and from each other, the push is refused, naming the path. No file's content is merged. One path is treated more strictly: where the candidate is a merge commit, BRANCH's `log.jsonl` must equal the ancestor's, and the push is refused if it does not, so the candidate's log is `main`'s. A BRANCH that carries entries of its own therefore cannot be pushed over a moved `main` by this clause; its builder brings it up to `main` first, as today. The push is also refused where the two commits have no merge base or more than one, where a path's value on either side is a gitlink (mode 160000), and where the chosen values would make one path both a file and a directory.
   - (c) *Every check is of the candidate.* The scan for filled trailers runs over the commits the candidate has and the base lacks. The digest comparison, the check, the test of whether a run of record is required, and that run are made on the candidate; the run's twelve parts each report `commit` equal to the candidate's commit, where today they report "BRANCH's head" (`RUNBOOK.md` lines 116–117). The read gate is given the candidate.
   - (d) *The read gate sees what is pushed.* The gate today takes the brief, the reviews and the bytes each review vouches for from `origin/main` (`tools/jack` line 187, `current = tree_bytes(repo, "origin/main", brief)`; `RUNBOOK.md` lines 128–133), and of the branch it compares `judge/scope.json` alone (line 180). So a branch that changes the brief is admitted today on a read of the brief that `main` holds. This clause adds one test, whether or not `main` has moved. When `--opens LAYER` is required, the candidate's bytes at `transcripts/BRIEF-LAYER-draft.md`, at `transcripts/windows/LAYER.json` where `origin/main` holds that file, and at each review the gate counts, must equal `origin/main`'s; if any differs the push is refused, naming the path. What was read is then what is pushed.
   - (e) *The evidence files.* The review and the run's parts are written under `transcripts/` after the checks, as today (`tools/jack` lines 237–241). They are not part of the tree that was checked, and the self-check runs with them in place. This clause adds two refusals, made before any of those files is written. One is of a name the verb would write — `transcripts/` followed by the basename of `--review`, or a part's name — that already exists in the candidate's tree. The other is of two such names that are equal, a review whose basename is a part's name: today the review would then replace the part on `main` while the entry's `record` is still computed from the part the verb made (`tools/jack` lines 237–244).
   - (f) *The effect.* Local `main`, which stands on the base, is fast-forwarded to the candidate, and the entry `push` is made on it as today. On any failure `main` returns to `origin/main` and BRANCH is untouched; a merge commit the verb made is then on no branch.

7. *The window.* Nothing in this clause permits a push that a window's rules refuse. R11 stands ("No verb fast-forwards a proposal onto `main` inside the window (`push` is refused there)") and so does criterion 3: a new attempt is not a second opening. Every attempt of `push` after the first refuses, before any check, if its base's log holds a `push` entry beyond the log of the first attempt's base; so a second opening that lands at any moment between the first attempt's fetch and a later attempt's fetch ends the verb. A merge commit made under point 6 is the pusher's commit. What it changes against its first parent is a push's change and never a proposal's, whatever trailers BRANCH's commits carry. That follows from R11 as written and from point 6 (b). R11 finds the proposal a merge on `main` brought "among the entries the second parent's log appends to the first parent's", and says that "a merge on `main` with no such entry ... brought no proposal and is a hand commit". By point 6 (b) the second parent's log, BRANCH's, equals the ancestor's and so appends nothing to the first parent's. R11 therefore finds no proposal in such a merge, and `evidence_provenance` counts what a push brings as nobody's (its twin 7).

**What it does not change.** No check is skipped, and none is remembered from an earlier attempt. An entry is lawful only on the prefix it finally lands on, and the kernel, the self-check and the judge decide that as they do today. No push is forced; no remote branch is deleted; no commit is rewritten. The verb merges no proposal and closes none. `merge` and every branch verb are as they were. BRANCH's ref never moves. A paste of the sovereign's needs no guard that waits for a quiet minute, and a guard still in a paste does no harm.

**What it does not establish.** That a push inside a window is lawful: point 7. That the files written under `transcripts/` after the checks were themselves checked: point 6 (e) keeps today's order and says so. That a moved `main` is a good `main`: the verb follows whatever the judge admitted. And case (d) of point 3 leaves one state the verb cannot decide — a push that failed while the remote could not be read — which it reports and does not guess at.
