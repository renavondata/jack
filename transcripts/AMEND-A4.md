## Amendment A4: the foundation is an incumbent (26 October 2026)

Counsel's text, proposed on 26 October 2026 and recorded by `amend` A4 under SPEC §7 after a
`decision` of kind `transmute` naming it, in force from its `activate_at`. Appended; overrides
earlier text where they conflict. Forced by: the record of the seven repositories that preceded
this one (`transcripts/PREDECESSORS-20261026.md`), none of which carried its own reason for
stopping, and whose replacement was in every case an unscored act of the owner; by forecast seq
53, which this amendment is the mechanism for; and, for clauses 2 and 7, by the reading of the
plan against the statistical literature (`transcripts/LITERATURE-20261026.md`).

1. **Incumbents.** Whatever is in force is an incumbent with a measured rate on the ledger: the
   trusted base at its digest, the clerk, a world, a proposer in a role (a model, counsel, Guy in
   his ordinary acts), a rule. Its rate is what §6's test of that thing measures — safety by
   replay, liveness by writ, value by proper score — over the entries of the last pre-stated
   window, and is read from the log, never asserted.
2. **Challengers.** A replacement for an incumbent enters only as a challenger: a `forecast` of
   type `challenger` by the party proposing it, naming the incumbent, the score, the window and
   the bound, entered before any text or code of the replacement reaches `main`. It is scored on
   the same ledger by §6's e-process. Where both can be scored on the same items — forecasters,
   clerks, worlds run in shadow — the test is paired: the exponentiated cumulative difference of
   clipped log scores (Henzi & Ziegel 2022; Choe & Ramdas 2024). Where they cannot, because the
   incumbent's rate comes from another period, the null is not the incumbent's measured rate but
   the upper end of an anytime-valid confidence sequence for it, its error pre-split from α. The
   challenger replaces the incumbent only when *E_t* ≥ 1/α within its window; otherwise the
   incumbent stands and the forecast resolves 0 against its proposer, and a challenger not measured
   by the window's end is resolved 0 by the clerk. For the trusted base and the charter the
   threshold is 1/α and never less; for a reversible incumbent a class may pre-state an
   expected-utility rule under the budget, which is itself an entrant, and until one is elected
   the threshold is the same.
3. **Founding texts.** A text that founds a new core, repository, ladder or charter is out of
   order unless it is a challenger's text under clause 2. From C1b's installing push the judge
   refuses a `push` that changes the trusted base, and a proposal adding a founding text, while
   the log holds no unresolved `challenger` forecast by that party naming what is replaced
   (pins `unforecast_push_admitted`, `challenger_never_resolved`, each with its passing twin).
   Until that push the rule binds by this text alone, and a breach is a precedent under §7.
4. **The sovereign is bound.** Clause 3 governs Guy's acts of founding and replacing as it
   governs every party's. A new repository or trusted base entered by him without a challenger
   forecast is a repair, recorded as a precedent case; the `push` verb's refusal is the mechanism,
   and his key is not exempt from it. The evidence for binding the sovereign here rather than the
   parties is the record: credence, life-agent, proplang, tannen, wald and assize were each
   replaced by his decision, with the reason written only in the successor's founding text.
5. **The bootstrap, as a pin.** §8 is read with this exit: the bootstrap is complete at the first
   reading of the clerk at which all hold — liveness *E_t* ≥ 20 against *p₀* on items completed
   with no repair by Guy (C1b); value *E_t* ≥ 20 for aggregated forecasts against the base-rate
   forecaster on at least one world whose outcomes are resolved by a mechanism outside the
   repository and carry a price or a market (C2); at least one invitation decided by the election
   without Guy; no challenger pending; procedure to object within §12's 5 : 1 in the last window;
   counsel not invited in that window. From that reading the foundation changes only by clause 2.
6. **The price of this amendment is recorded.** A4 is the fourth amendment in thirteen days; §12's
   guard applies. Counsel's forecasts: entered before this text (seq 53), jack is the foundation
   in force on 26 January 2027, p 0.6; entered with it (seq 60), clause 3 refuses, or is cited as
   the forcing entry against, at least one founding text or trusted-base push by 26 January 2027,
   p 0.5. A4 lapses as a rule guarding a rare event lapses: not by a count of quiet windows, but
   when an e-process on its firing rate, against the rate clause 6 forecasts, reaches 1/α after
   C1b's promotion; and it is tested under §3 as any rule is.
7. **The wealth is never reset, and the bet is mechanical.** §6 is read with two sentences the
   literature forces (`transcripts/LITERATURE-20261026.md`, items 2 and 5). First: a window that
   fails and is followed by a new one continues the same wealth process, or the new window's α is
   pre-split from the layer's; *E* is never set back to 1, since *k* fresh starts against one bound
   are *k* chances at it. Second: the betting fraction λᵢ is a deterministic function of the log
   prefix — the prequential Laplace estimate of the rate, truncated strictly inside (0, 1/p₀) —
   so predictability is mechanical, no λ is ever entered after its outcome, and λ = 1/p₀, which a
   single adverse outcome sends to ruin, is never used.
