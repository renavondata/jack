## Amendment A4: the foundation is an incumbent (26 October 2026)

Counsel's text, second version, proposed on 26 October 2026 after the cold read
`transcripts/REVIEW-A4.md` (BLOCK) and answered in `transcripts/REPLY-REVIEW-A4.md`; recorded by

1. **Incumbents are what a brief has measured.** An incumbent is anything in force whose class
   has, in a brief in force, a pre-stated statistic: the population of entries it is computed
   over, the window, and the score — §6's liveness or value test for that class. The trusted base
   at its digest, the clerk, a world, a proposer in a role and a rule are such classes once a brief
   states their statistic; a class with none cannot be challenged or replaced until it has one.
   Safety (§6's replay) is a gate that incumbent and challenger alike must pass; it is never the
   score on which they are compared, since a score every safe thing attains at 1 can never be
   beaten.
2. **Challengers.** A replacement for an incumbent enters only as a challenger: a `forecast` of
   type `challenger` by the party proposing it, naming the incumbent, the class's statistic, the
   window and the bound, entered before any text or code of the replacement reaches `main`. The
   comparison is §6's e-process on the class's statistic, with the null stated for the data at
   hand: where incumbent and challenger are scored on the same items (forecasters; a clerk or a
   world run in shadow), the null is that the challenger's expected score is no better, tested as
   a betting e-process on the bounded difference, *E_t* = ∏(1 + λᵢ Dᵢ / B), with Dᵢ the
   challenger's score minus the incumbent's on item *i*, B the bound the clipping fixes (ln 999
   for per-mille log loss), and predictable λᵢ ∈ [0, 1); where they are not, the null rate is the
   upper end of a confidence sequence for the incumbent's rate at the layer's α, the challenger
   is tested against it at that same α, and the recorded guarantee is 2α, said so in the forecast.
   A challenger wins when *E_t* ≥ 1/α within its window; one not measured by the window's end is
   resolved 0 by the clerk.
3. **Reversible replacements are priced; irreversible ones are gated** (§2.4). A reversible
   incumbent — a proposer, a clerk heuristic, a world, a rule outside the trusted base — is
   replaced by its challenger at once for the window, scored as above, and reverts at the window's
   end unless the challenger has won; nothing waits. The trusted base and `CHARTER.md` are gated:
   a `push` that changes the trusted-base digest, or a proposal that changes `CHARTER.md`,
   `SPEC.md`, `LAW.md` or `judge/scope.json` or adds a `BRIEF-*.md` that opens a layer, is refused
   by the judge from C1b's installing push unless its body names a `challenger` forecast by the
   same party that has resolved 1 and whose `args` name the digest or file being entered
   (pins `unwon_push_admitted`, `unwon_founding_admitted`, `safety_used_as_score`, each with a
   passing twin). Until that push the rule binds by this text alone and a breach is a precedent
   under §7. A repository founded outside this one is beyond any layer's refusal; the rule reaches
   it by record only: such a founding without a challenger forecast is a repair, recorded as a
   precedent, and nothing more is claimed for it.
4. **The sovereign is bound, as a bet.** Clause 3 governs Guy's `push` and founding proposals as
   it governs every party's; his key is not exempt from the judge's refusal. The evidence for
   binding him is narrower than a history of decisions: of the eight predecessors read, none
   records in its own repository the decision that ended it (credence-governor's retired shadow
   the honest exception), and the reason for this repository is written in this repository and
   not in assize. Whether the rule changes anything is forecast seq 53, p 0.6, and nothing here
   asserts more.
5. **The bootstrap, decidable.** §8 is read with this exit for C0–C2, which does not touch C3's
   test: the bootstrap is complete at a `decision` of kind `promote` with subject `bootstrap`
   whose body carries, and whose checks recompute from the log alone, that liveness *E_t* ≥ 1/α
   against *p₀* for items completed with no repair by the master key (C1b's statistic); that value
   *E_t* ≥ 1/α for the aggregated forecast against the base-rate forecaster on at least one world
   of a type whose brief declares its outcomes resolved by a mechanism outside the repository
   (C2's statistic, defined in C2's brief); that every `invite` entry of the last window names the
   proposer the election drew; that no `challenger` forecast is unresolved; and that procedure to
   object in the last window is within §12's bound. From that decision the foundation changes
   only by clauses 2 and 3.
6. **The wealth is never reset, and the bet is mechanical.** §6 is read with two sentences the
   literature forces. First: a window that fails and is followed by a new one continues the same
   wealth process on the same statistic; *E* is never set back to 1, since *k* fresh starts
   against one bound are *k* chances at it, and no α is split. Second: for a binary statistic
   the betting fraction is λᵢ = max(0, (q̂ᵢ − p₀) / (p₀(1 − p₀))), with q̂ᵢ = (sᵢ + 1)/(nᵢ + 2) the
   Laplace estimate over all outcomes of that statistic since the layer's first window; q̂ᵢ < 1
   strictly, so λᵢ < 1/p₀ with no further constant, λᵢ is a function of the prefix alone, and no
   λ is ever entered after its outcome. For a bounded difference (clause 2) the same q̂ is taken
   on the sign of Dᵢ and λᵢ = max(0, 2q̂ᵢ − 1).
