## 0. The law of this window is a file

`transcripts/windows/C2c-2.json` is this window, as BRIEF-C2c-1 §0 made a window its file: its opening rule, preconditions, promotion forecast, end, numbers, processes, frozen paths, criteria with the evidence each reads, reads, and what is reserved to the sovereign. `tools/status.py` assesses C2c-2 from that file and from nothing in this prose. Where this brief and the file differ, the file governs and the difference is a finding. The file is bound as C2c-1's is: it is final and read before the opening sitting; the sovereign's `decision budget "C2c-2: window file sha256:<hash>; …"` names its hash; counsel's promotion forecast names the same hash; and `status.py` refuses the whole assessment when the file at the assessed prefix does not hash to both.

## 1. Purpose

Three things reach the record that today live in a chat or in a habit. The sovereign's rulings, directions and reads become entries naming bytes (R1). A text that claims force reaches `main` only with a read by another principal and another family, and a push only with each party's part signed for by its own key (R2, R3). And the head of the log is published where the owner cannot quietly rewrite it (R4). The window also carries two things the sovereign directed on 17 November: how a reader's BLOCK is ended (R5), and, for every obligation still kept by hand, what follows its breach and where it is to go (R6).

## 3. Rules (new in C2c-2)

Each rule's text is in the file named; this brief adds the criterion that tests it in the window and says what the criterion cannot show.

### R1 The sovereign's acts name bytes

The rule is `transcripts/SPEC-PATCH-c2c-2-draft.md` §1: the `decision` kind `wish`, its subject, the clerk's `W6` line, read lists and the prefix mark.

**Criterion kind `wishes_named`.** The window file's `wishes` is a list of paths: `transcripts/RULING-20261104-window-fix.md`, `transcripts/RULING-20261117-constitution.md` and `transcripts/COUNSEL-OPEN.md`. The criterion is `met` when, for each, the log at the assessed prefix holds a `wish` entered inside the window, by the key ground 14 admits, that names the path with bytes the path held at some commit of `main`'s first-parent history — directly or through a read list — and, for the two rulings, with the bytes the path holds at the assessed tree. It reads the log and the tree only and is never `unknown`.

*What it shows and does not.* That the kind works on the real log from his key through both kernels, the judge and the clerk, and that his two rulings are signed for as they stand. It does not show that he read anything: a wish is his statement.

### R2 A text that claims force carries a read

The rule is the same file's §2: ground 15, `unread_text`, with the `read` list.

**Criterion kind `covered_reads_replayed`.** For every commit on `main`'s first-parent history inside the window that is the merge of a proposal and at which a path covered by the `read` list in force differs from the first parent, the criterion runs ground 15 as the judge on `main` at the assessed prefix states it, with the first parent as trunk and the second as the proposal, and requires that it admit. It also requires that at least one such merge exists (the window file's `covered_changes_min`, 1). It is `unmet` when any replay refuses, when a first-parent commit that is no merge of a proposal changes a covered path, or when there is no such merge.

*What it shows and does not.* That no covered text reached `main` in the window round the judge, and that the ground admitted at least one text with a real read: the runbook's and the case index's changes that the patch's §4 orders after the push are such texts. It shows nothing about the quality of a read.

### R3 A push takes its facts from keys

### R4 The published head, the witnessed log and the verifier

The rule is `transcripts/runner/monitor.md` as brought to answer 6 (draft v6): publication of every head by jack's own workflow; each published line entered in a Sigsum log under a named policy, with its proof kept; a verifier that recomputes every line, checks every proof and every leaf, and files a writ on any failure; and the same verifier run a second time from a forge that is not GitHub.

**Criterion kind `head_published`** (evidence `monitor`). At every verifier run inside the window, `main`'s head had a line in `HASHES` or was younger than the lag; no `publish.yml` run inside the window concluded other than `success`; and the workflow's state was `active` at every copy of the evidence. `unknown (no monitor)` without the evidence; `unknown (stale monitor)` when its newest copy is older than the file's staleness rule.

**Criterion kind `verifier_record`** (evidence `monitor`). The newest line of the first instance's record is `ok`, within twenty-four hours of the assessed instant, from a GitHub-hosted run; the newest line of the second instance's record is `ok` within the same bound; every failure line of either since the opening names an issue the evidence holds; and at least one line of each since the opening shows steps 8 and 9 run, with every proof accepted and no leaf without a line.

*What they show and do not.* That heads were published, logged and verified while the window ran, by code whose bytes the court holds, on two hosts that are not the owner's. ### R5 A BLOCK suspends, the sovereign rules, and the reader is scored

This is the direction of answer 3 (`transcripts/QUESTION-20261111-constitution.md` §2.3 c; his words of 11 November, COUNSEL-OPEN row 57: "Yes, I end disagreements"). **It sets a lower bar than the earlier briefs set: his ruling can stand in for a reader's acceptance.** It binds by this brief once he has read it, from the merge of the proposal that carries its code, and so first at C2c-3's opening. It does not reach an opening made before that. It changes the opening gate in `tools/jack` and nothing in the judge: ground 15 stays as the SPEC patch states it.

**What the gate is today.** `push_read_gate` passes on the first review of the brief that is accepting, at `main`'s bytes, and brought by a principal other than counsel; from C2c-1's opening, for a layer with a window file, it requires as many such reviews as the file's `readers` says, each at the window file's bytes too (RUNBOOK §`jack push`). A failing review beside them refuses nothing in code. "Until two families accept" has been kept by hand.

**The rule.**

1. *A blocking ground has a form.* A review of a brief or a window file whose first line is `VERDICT: FAIL` carries each blocking ground as one line, `Block: <id>; pin <name>; p <n>` or `Block: <id>; clause <cite>; p <n>`. `<id>` is unique in the file. `<n>` is the reader's probability, per mille from 1 to 999, that the ground is not overruled. A failing review with no `Block:` line, or with one of another form, is a failing review that cannot be overruled.
2. *The reader forecasts.* The session that proposes the review enters, in the same proposal, one `forecast` for each `Block:` line: type `review.block`, `args` `{model, review, id}` with `review` the file's path and the SHA-256 of its bytes, `p` as the line states it, and `resolves_by` the window file's `block_days` after the entry. The claim is that no ruling of the sovereign's on the log at `resolves_by` overrules the ground. A resolver resolves it 0 if the log at `resolves_by` holds a `wish` naming a `transcripts/RULING-*.md` file that carries the line `Overruled: <review> <id>`, and 1 otherwise. A ground the drafter concedes by changing the text therefore resolves 1, and every such forecast can be resolved on its day.
3. *The gate counts acceptances and admits no standing BLOCK.* `jack push --opens LAYER` requires the window file's `readers` qualifying reviews, each at `main`'s bytes of the brief and of the window file, and no review at those bytes that fails and is not overruled. A review qualifies when it is accepting, or when it fails and every `Block:` line of it is overruled.
4. *Overruled.* A ground is overruled when a `transcripts/RULING-*.md` file in `main`'s tree carries `Overruled: <review> <id>`, and a `wish` on the log names that file's bytes. The ruling gives his reasons and is itself a covered text (ground 15). An answered ground is not read again at the same bytes: the line names the review's bytes, and the review names the text's.
5. *What is not his to overrule.* A `Block:` line whose clause is on the window file's `reserved_clauses` list cannot be overruled, and an `Overruled:` line naming it has no effect. The list is the rules that bind him: CHARTER §2 maxim 1, A2.6, A2.7, A2.10 and A5.1. Such a ground stands until a reader accepts new bytes or a decidable check answers it (the paper: "a decidable check answers it, or it stands").
6. *The score.* `status.py` prints, for each (principal, model) pair, its count and mean log score on resolved `review.block` forecasts. It is printed and is no criterion of this window. Whether C2c-3's draw of readers reads it is that brief's to say.

**What this does not do.** It does not let a `decision` dispose of a finding: the instrument is a ruling file read under ground 15 and named by a `wish`, and the gate reads lines, not prose. It does not reach reviews of code (the push's checks, SPEC patch §3) or reviews under ground 15. It does not make him the judge of a ground about his own compliance. It does not score a reader for being agreed with: a reader who blocks rightly and is conceded to scores as one who is upheld.

**Pin `block_suspends`.** Twins: (1) two accepting reviews at the bytes → the gate passes; (2) two accepting and one failing, not overruled → refused; (3) the failing one overruled in every `Block:` line by a ruling a wish names → passes; (4) one of its lines not overruled → refused; (5) the ruling on `main` and no wish naming its bytes → refused; (6) a `Block:` on a reserved clause, overruled → refused; (7) one accepting and one failing review, the failing one overruled → passes with `readers` 2; (8) a failing review with no `Block:` line → refused whatever rulings exist; (9) an `Overruled:` line naming other bytes of the review → refused. Mutants: one for each twin, dropping the test it names.

### R7 Each party's key is its own

The rule is the custody text (`transcripts/runner/custody.md`, in draft): one user on the host for each party, holding that party's key and no other; one runner group for each, restricted to the workflow files and commits `tools/runner/custody.json` lists; `run.yml` taking a session's party from the issue's label and no key from its body. It is row c of R6.

**Its criterion is a row of the ratchet (criterion 8).** The row `custody` reads `hand` at the opening tree and `code` at the assessed one, and its witness is the operation of the custody text's §4, observed inside the window and copied into the runner evidence: a builder's session that is refused the reviewer's key by the host and leaves no entry signed by another party's key on any branch; a workflow pushed to a branch of the runner that names the reviewer's group and never starts; and each group's workflow list, read through the API, equal to `custody.json`. The row is `unmet` when any of the three is absent or stale.

*What it shows and does not.* That on the day of the evidence a session could not sign as another party and a pushed workflow could not run as one. It shows nothing about the owner, who is root on the host and owns the organisation, and nothing about what a party's session does with its own key.
