# Question paper, 11 November 2026: what binds the sovereign between amendments, and how the charter is made enforceable

Counsel-5's paper (`claude-fable-5-2`), put to the sovereign under A2.10. **It binds nothing.** It answers his direction of 11 November ("I think there's a 'constitutional' question arising here that needs to be dealt with") and his later ones the same day ("research how to make the charter enforceable and how and why"; "So let's implement your findings"). Each option below has been set against the charter before it is offered; two cold reads (runner issues #678 and #679) found seven faults in the first draft and one in the second, and this is the paper after them. Counsel holds forecasts that need C2a-prime to open (V5–V7), and its predecessor drafted the entry at seq 3432 whose words §2.1 finds to have no effect; both interests run against the recommendations here.

**Three kinds of answer are asked, and they are not the same instrument.**
- A **reading** (R) is his ruling on what the charter as it stands allows (A2.10). It adds nothing to the charter. It is recorded as a `RULING-*.md` file with its reasons, which is "the record of Guy's answer" and is followed (correction 4 of the ruling of 4 November) until it is carried into the charter by amendment, as A2.10's "recorded here" requires; where a judgment and the charter differ, the charter stands (`CASES.md`).
- A **direction** (D) is his wish as to how counsel drafts briefs and texts from now on. It binds no party until a brief he has read pre-states it for its layer (A2.4: a brief may tighten the charter's bounds, never loosen them).
- **Amendment material** (A) is what only charter text can do. It is prepared for his `transmute` and `amend` (A2.7) and binds from its height, never before.

None of the seven answers of §5 is mechanically decidable as put: readings and directions are prose. Their decidable forms are C2c-2's texts — the `wish` shape, the check at the push, the monitor's record.

**Sources.** Two research reports made for him on 11 November in counsel's conversation, which could not open this repository's files; counsel's supplement on constitutional monarchies and on projects that govern agents (the sovereign's Project, `claude/constitutional-question-supplement-20261111.md`); and this repository, against which counsel has checked what the reports say of jack (§4). ## 1. The case, and the principle proposed

On 4 November window code was written before the sovereign had read its text: a breach of A2.10, which had no enforcer and states no consequence. On 11 November he entered a `decision` of kind `grant` (seq 3432) that stated his read and the breach, said the breach "stands, listed", that the re-cut "may enter by the opening push", and that the gate reads' findings "are answered by this entry". A reviewer then blocked: "a later grant cannot loosen the charter". He chose to have the code written again after his read ("Yes, b, that's the price").

**The principle, as amendment material.** *No entry has a power the law in force at its parent did not give it, the sovereign's included; and every rule that is not refused at admission states, before it can be broken, what follows its breach.* The first half is the Case of Proclamations (1610) and the Bill of Rights (1689, art. 1) as a rule of recognition; §2.1 reads it as already the law, and the amendment would only say so in terms. The second half is new: it is the "or else" that separates a rule from a norm in Crawford and Ostrom's grammar, and the charter does not have it.

## 2. The five points

### 2.1 Dispensation: can a `decision` suspend a rule for a case, or make a past breach lawful?

| | Option | For | Against | Lawful? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a | **No.** A `decision` has the effect the law in force gives its kind, and no other. What else it says is his signed statement: evidence of a fact, or of a choice he makes within powers he otherwise has | the only reading that can later bind an agent with no back door; costs nothing | a true emergency waits for A2.7's two acts | A reading, and the law as it stands: SPEC §8 gives no `decision` but `transmute` an effect on the state; the charter changes only by A2.7's path; no clause gives any entry a power to suspend a rule or to make a past breach lawful |
| b | A bounded waiver power, granted in advance: named rule, named case, reason, expiry, priced as a repair | exceptions become lawful and visible | waivers become habit; repairs get re-typed as waivers (§12); no case has needed one | Only by amendment, for the future; it could not reach A2.7 itself, a rule guarding an irreversible effect, or a rule that binds its author |
| c | A later amendment that makes the writing of 4 November lawful | — | the law looks forward (§3) | No: an amendment is in force from its height, "never over earlier entries" (A2.7). An amendment can only say, for the future, what follows a breach (§2.2) |

Recommended: **a**, with b refused until a case shows the need. Of seq 3432 on that reading: it is a valid entry. Its words on his read, on the breach, that the breach stands listed, that it is no precedent and that the eight obligations stand are his signed statements — evidence, and no exercise of a power. "May enter by the opening push" and "are answered by this entry" have no effect in law: no kind of `decision` admits code or disposes of another party's finding. Nothing is struck from the log, and his choice of 11 November has made the first of the two moot. The UK Supreme Court treated an Order in Council beyond the prerogative's limits the same way in 2019: a blank sheet. Forecast that he reads it so: 0.85.

### 2.5 The read: which texts does A2.10 cover, and what proves a read?

**Scope, an addition.** A2.10 says "Code is written to a text Guy has read" and fixes no scope for "a text". Three candidates: (a) every byte the code touches — impossible; (b) **every text that carries a rule or an acceptance criterion the code is written to** — a brief's rule-bearing sections, the specification of its pins, a bound such as the counted cap — with tests and pin code tracing to such a text; (c) only the sections a brief lists, which is how §The window, §The subject and §Pins of BRIEF-C2a-prime came to be built to with no record of a read. A brief's list cannot make code written to an unread text keep A2.10 (A2.4: a brief does not loosen), so c is not open. Two cold reads (#678, #679) hold that b is an addition to A2.10 and no reading of it. It is therefore asked as a direction and as amendment material, and not as a ruling on the charter's words; nothing practical turns on the label, since b is the stricter course and counsel follows it already.

**Proof, an addition.** A2.10 names no form for a read. That it be **an entry of his naming bytes**, and that *a read of bytes that extend bytes already read is a read of the extension* (a prefix test, by which a brief's appended amendments are read without reading the brief again), are not readings of A2.10 either. Scope b, the entry and the prefix rule are lawful now in two ways: as counsel's own procedure for filing sessions, and as a brief's tightening for its layer; they bind generally only by the amendment. A reader in his place is a regency: amendment material, deferred.

**Enforcer.** Today the builder's own refusal (the templates of #780: a session prints his decisions and writes nothing to a text none names) — a prompt, not a check. From C2c-2, on a direction, a check at the push by commit ancestry: code commits descend from the commit on `main` that carries the entry; the monitor replays it. Until `wish` exists, a `grant` in prose is his signed statement of a read, as at seq 3432. Forecast that he takes b, the entry and the prefix rule: 0.8.

## 3. Enforcement: the standard, and where each finding goes

**The ladder, a direction for drafting.** A clause is enforced at one of six rungs: impossible by construction (keys, capabilities, shapes); refused at admission (kernel, judge); gated before an irreversible step; detected afterwards at a pre-stated price; attested by a party other than the one bound; narrated by hand. Validity belongs at admission, where refusal "costs the proposer nothing but resubmission" (A2.3); irreversible effects behind a gate; reversible conduct priced (maxim 4); merit is a forecast (§3). The direction asked: every obligation a brief leaves at the last rung carries, in that brief or in the enforcement map, its target rung and the window that delivers it, or counsel proposes striking it. Enforcement by code is also the only kind that spends none of his attention for each act.

**Against the sovereign himself** the reachable aim is tamper-evidence, not prevention: he administers GitHub and is root on steel, so any rule there can be lifted by him; a public head record cosigned by witnesses he does not run cannot be quietly rewritten. Prevention needs a second key-holder and belongs to the layer at which an adversarial agent arrives.

**A2.1 and the rule language, a reading.** A2.1 has the language of §3 enter "at the layer in which the first real case arises: the first entry Guy wants refused that recognition does not refuse", and has each layer's report say whether one arose. Seq 3432 raises the question, and only he can answer it: does he want such an entry *refused*? Counsel's reading is no: by §2.1 it is a valid entry whose surplus words have no effect, and an entry of no effect needs no refusing; so no first real case has arisen, and C2a's report says so. If he does want such entries refused, then on A2.1's words the rule language enters at this layer, with its cost in kernel lines and a push. The reports advise against building it now (Gerrit replaced rules in Prolog with boolean expressions over a fixed vocabulary); C2c-2's `wish` narrows what a subject may say by an ordinary SPEC change, which is not a rule language.
