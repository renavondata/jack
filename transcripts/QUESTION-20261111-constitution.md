## 1. The case, and the principle proposed

On 4 November window code was written before the sovereign had read its text: a breach of A2.10, which had no enforcer and states no consequence. On 11 November he entered a `decision` of kind `grant` (seq 3432) that attested his read, named the breach, let it stand listed, said the re-cut "may enter by the opening push" and that the gate reads' findings "are answered by this entry". A reviewer then blocked: "a later grant cannot loosen the charter". He chose a rebuild after his read ("Yes, b, that's the price").

**The principle.** *No entry has a power the law in force at its parent did not give it, the sovereign's included; and every rule that is not refused at admission states, before it can be broken, what follows its breach.* The first half is the Case of Proclamations (1610) and the Bill of Rights (1689, art. 1) as a rule of recognition, and is what §3 and A2.7 already say. The second is the "or else" that separates a rule from a norm in Crawford and Ostrom's grammar; maxim 4 already cites Ostrom's graduated sanctions.

### 2.5 The read: which texts does A2.10 cover, and what proves a read?

Scope: (a) every byte code touches — impossible; (b) **every text that carries a rule or an acceptance criterion code is written to**: a brief's rule-bearing sections, the specification of its pins, a bound such as the counted cap, with tests and pin code tracing to such a text; (c) only the sections a brief lists — which is how §The window, §The subject and §Pins of BRIEF-C2a-prime came to be built to with no record of a read. A brief may tighten A2.10 and not narrow it (A2.4), so c is not open.

Proof: a chat message transcribed by counsel fails the corollary of maxim 4 (no party acts except through entries that carry its identity); **an entry of his naming bytes** passes it. A hash names a file, not a section, so a read is of a file at stated bytes, and *a read of bytes that extend bytes already read is a read of the extension* — decidable by a prefix test, and how a brief's appended amendments are read without reading the brief again. A reader in his place is a regency: an amendment, deferred.

Enforcer: today the builder's own refusal (the templates of #780: a session prints his decisions and writes nothing to a text none names); from C2c-2 a check at the push by commit ancestry — code commits descend from the commit on `main` that carries the entry — replayed by the monitor. Recommended: **b, the entry, the prefix rule, those two enforcers.** Until `wish` exists, a `grant` in prose stands in, as at seq 3432. Forecast: 0.8.

## 3. Enforcement: the standard, and where each finding goes

**The ladder.** A clause is enforced at one of six rungs: impossible by construction (keys, capabilities, shapes); refused at admission (kernel, judge); gated before an irreversible step; detected afterwards at a pre-stated price; attested by a party other than the one bound; narrated by hand. Validity belongs at admission, where refusal "costs the proposer nothing but resubmission" (A2.3); irreversible effects behind a gate; reversible conduct priced (maxim 4); merit is a forecast (§3). A clause left at the last rung is a debt with a named target and window, or it is struck. Enforcement by code is also the only kind that spends none of his attention for each act.

**Against the sovereign himself** the reachable standard is tamper-evidence, not prevention: he administers GitHub and is root on steel, so any rule there can be lifted by him; a public head record cosigned by witnesses he does not run cannot be quietly rewritten. Prevention needs a second key-holder and belongs to the layer at which an adversarial agent arrives.

**The rule language.** A2.1 has the language of §3 enter "at the layer in which the first real case arises: the first entry Guy wants refused that recognition does not refuse", and has each layer's report say whether one arose. Seq 3432 is arguably that entry. Counsel's reading, for his ruling: the case is answered by widening recognition — a `decision`'s subject takes the shape its kind allows, so that a power cannot be claimed in prose — and not by a rule language, which stays dormant until an untrusted party must author rules. Gerrit's history supports this: it replaced rules in Prolog with boolean expressions over a fixed vocabulary. C2a's report states the case and this reading either way.
