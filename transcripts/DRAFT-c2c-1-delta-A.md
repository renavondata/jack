## Amendment 1 (18 November, counsel-5; criterion 10 asks for one undertaking from the opening inventory, due and kept inside the window — before the opening, one clause)

**Why.** The audit (`transcripts/AUDIT-criteria-20261117.md`, C2c-1 row 10) classes criterion 10 as "**empty** as an in-window delivery test when no undertaking falls due before exit": counsel can match every open row with a forecast due after the exit, keep none, and the criterion reads `met`. The first draft of this amendment required one undertaking due inside the window and kept. Its reader then named the weakest system that still passed: one undertaking that costs nothing to keep, resolved 1 by another party, due inside the window, with the forecast, what it promised and its resolution all entered before the opening (runner #1012). The second draft's reader found that it spoke of the row's `due` where its clause tests the undertaking's `resolves_by`, and that its account of what cannot be repaired was wrong (runner #1018).

**The clause.** Criterion 10 is also `unmet` unless the undertakings that meet all five of these carry between them at least `undertakings_due_min` different `args.id`s:
1. it is counsel's forecast of type `counsel.undertaking` with `args.window` `C2c-1`;
2. its `args.id` is the `id` of a row that is open in the inventory at the opening tree ("no `done` and no `closed`", R5) and due after the opening push's `ts` — one of the rows that R5 has counsel re-enter before the push;
3. its own entry precedes the opening push;
4. its `resolves_by` is at or before the window's stated end, and at or before the assessed instant;
5. it is resolved 1 by an entry that lies inside the window: after the opening push, and at or before its `resolves_by`.

The window file's `numbers` gains `undertakings_due_min: 1`; criterion 10's `args` gains `"due_min": "undertakings_due_min"` and its `text` gains this clause; and R1's list of numeric `args` gains `due_min`, a `numbers` key like the others. Nothing else of R5 or of the criterion changes. The clause can only turn `met` into `unmet`: it tightens, as row 70 records ("Counsel tightens the C2c-1 draft's criteria 10 and 12 in its delta before his read").

**What it shows.** At the assessed prefix no undertaking of counsel's that is past due is unkept (R5, unchanged), and at least one undertaking that counsel entered before the opening, for a row that stood open in its inventory when the window opened, has fallen due inside the window and been resolved 1, in time, by a party other than counsel, in an entry made inside the window.

**What it does not show.** That the obligation was worth owing, or that what was done for it is of use. Nor that the work was done inside the window: what the undertaking promised may have been done before the opening, and the clause places only the undertaking's deadline and its resolution inside the window. The inventory is counsel's file, and a row says what counsel wrote. Of the system the first draft's reader named, the clause closes two parts: the resolution must be entered inside the window, and the undertaking must name a row of the inventory at the opening tree. It leaves the third: counsel can write into the inventory, before the opening, a row that costs nothing to keep, and an undertaking for that row then meets the clause. Counsel knows no test on the log that tells a weighty row from an empty one; that is a report of its search, not a proof. The check on that is outside this criterion: by R5 "the sovereign's check is one `git show` of the file at the opening tree".

**What follows from it.** Three things.
- *What must fall inside the window.* It is the undertaking's `resolves_by`, which counsel chose when it entered the forecast, and not the row's `due`: R5 has the row re-entered with "`resolves_by` at or before its `due`", so a row due after the window's end can meet the clause through a forecast due inside it. The clause does not require the inventory to hold a row due inside the window.
- *The wait.* The criterion cannot read `met` before the assessed instant has reached the `resolves_by` of undertakings carrying `undertakings_due_min` different ids (condition 4), however early they were resolved. The first prefix at which a promotion is lawful is therefore the first, at or after those deadlines, at which every other criterion also holds. The wait is as long as counsel made it when it chose the deadlines. The brief still states no minimum length and no second end (A5.2).
- *What cannot be repaired.* Only an undertaking entered before the opening push can count (condition 3), so whether the minimum can be met is fixed when the push is entered. If the undertakings that then meet conditions 1 to 3, are unresolved, and have a `resolves_by` after the push and at or before the stated end carry fewer than `undertakings_due_min` different ids, criterion 10 reads `unmet` at every prefix of the window and the window cannot be promoted. That can be read from the log and the opening tree at the push. After the opening, each such undertaking that fails — resolved 0, resolved after its `resolves_by`, or left unresolved past it — is lost to the minimum, and by R5 as it stood before this amendment it also reads criterion 10 `unmet` at every later prefix (old twins 2, 3 and 5), whatever caused the failure.

**Pin `undertakings_are_forecasts`.** The old twins 1, 4, 6 and 12 expect `met`. Each keeps that expectation only on a fixture that also holds an undertaking meeting the five conditions, and each gains a second form without one, expecting `unmet`.

## Amendment 2 (18 November, counsel-5; criterion 12 counts as object only what was declared and resolved inside the window — before the opening, one clause)

**Why.** The audit (the same file, C2c-1 row 12) classes criterion 12 as "**empty** as a check that substantive object work outweighs procedure; it establishes a label-count ratio": forecasts of any `world.` label, unanswered and with deadlines past the window, count as object, and an undeclared type prints a line and still counts. The second draft of this amendment made an undeclared forecast inside the window fail the criterion. Its reader showed that such a failure could not be repaired, since the entry stays on the log and the file is frozen (runner #1018). This draft counts such a forecast on neither side.

**The clause.** For criterion 12 in this window, every entry inside the window — after the opening push, on the assessed prefix — is of one of three kinds:
- *object*: (i) a `forecast` of a type that `transcripts/world-types.json` declares, whose `resolution` is on the assessed prefix; and (ii) that `resolution`;
- *counted on neither side*: any other `forecast` of a type beginning `world.`, and any other `resolution` of a forecast of such a type. These are a forecast of a declared type that is unresolved at the assessed prefix; a forecast of a type the file does not declare, with its resolution if it has one; and the resolution of a forecast that was entered before the opening push;
- *procedure*: every other entry, as A3.4 has it ("every other entry").

A type is not declared when it is absent from the file, or when the file holds it without one of the three fields or with a field of the wrong shape (R8 prints `undeclared: <type>`); and no type is declared when the file's bytes do not hash to `world_types.sha256` (R8 prints `undeclared: transcripts/world-types.json`). Those lines are printed as before, and the criterion's value depends on an undeclared forecast only in that it is not counted. The bound is unchanged: the criterion is `met` when the procedure count, with the exit `promote` counted in it, is at most `ratio_max` times the object count.

Draft v20 has "object as A3.4 reads it today", and A3.4 has "object entries (forecasts and resolutions of world types)". `tools/status.py` today (`main` `3d13088a`, line 186) counts as object every forecast inside the window of a type beginning `world.`, and every resolution inside the window of such a forecast, whenever that forecast was entered. Against that count the clause moves entries from object to neither side, and moves none into object and none into or out of procedure. So it can only lower the object count or leave it, and can only turn `met` into `unmet`: it tightens (A2.4). R8's second printed line is this clause's count with the types whose `scheduled_by` is not `none` removed from object; it is printed and not tested, as before.

**What it shows.** That inside the window the forecasts of declared types which were answered, with their answers, number at least a fifth of the procedure entries, counted as A3.4 counts them with the exit `promote` among them.

**What it does not show.** That a forecast was worth making, or that its claim has anything to do with the type it carries. The clause reads a forecast's `type` and whether it was resolved; it does not read the claim. The reader of the first draft named the system that still passes: content-free claims entered under a declared type, each truthfully resolved by another party (a claim that two and two make five, resolved 0). Each such pair adds two object entries, and enough of them outweigh any count of procedure. Counsel looked for a test on the log that would refuse them and spare honest work, and its search found none; that is a report of a search, not a proof. On `main`'s log the 45 forecasts of `world.hkjc` fall on 21 pairs of meeting and race; in 12 of the pairs counsel and the reviewer each forecast, and two of them, which are whole meetings, hold four different claims each (seqs 73 to 90). Counting one forecast for each meeting and race would discard real ones. The price of the padding is one resolution by another party for each forecast, and the entries are on the log for anyone to read. Nor does the clause say that a scheduled stream is not object: that is reserved to the sovereign for C2c-3 (`reserved_to_the_sovereign`, row 37), and the second printed line is what he reads for it.

**What can fail, and what can recover.** Counsel finds nothing in the clause that fails beyond repair before the window ends. A forecast of a declared type counts from the prefix at which its resolution is on the log, however late that resolution comes: the clause has no condition that a resolution be on time, so one delayed by an outage counts when it arrives. Whether a late resolution fails another criterion is that criterion's matter. What cannot be made up is time: a forecast whose resolution is not on the log when the window ends never counts in this window, so forecasts whose `resolves_by` lies after the end, or an outage that lasts to the end, can leave the object count short. A forecast of an undeclared type changes the printed line and no count. Procedure entries stay counted; a surplus of them is outweighed only by one further object entry for every five.

**Pin `object_declared`.** The old twins 1 and 3 expect a type's forecasts to be counted; each keeps that expectation only where the forecasts are entered inside the window and resolved at the assessed prefix. Twins 2, 5 and 6 keep their printed `undeclared` line; what such a forecast does to the counts is in twins 10 to 12 below.

The added twins share one fixture, G. The opening push is at *T*; the assessed prefix is the log up to *T* + 3 d and holds no `promote`; the file declares `world.renavon` with its three fields well-shaped, and its bytes hash to `world_types.sha256`; `ratio_max` is 5. Inside the window the log holds one forecast of `world.renavon`, resolved by another party, and nine other entries, none of them a forecast or a resolution of a type beginning `world.`, among them a `counsel.undertaking` forecast and its resolution. So object is 2 and procedure 9, which is 10 with the exit `promote`. G′ is G with one more such procedure entry: 11 with the exit `promote`.
- (7) G → `met`, 10 against 2;
- (8) G′ → `unmet`, 11 against 2;
- (9) G and G′, each with a further forecast of `world.renavon` inside the window that is unresolved at the assessed prefix → the counts of 7 and of 8, `met` and `unmet`;
- (10) G and G′, each with a forecast inside the window of `world.padding`, a type the file does not hold, resolved by another party → the counts of 7 and of 8, `met` and `unmet`, and `undeclared: world.padding` printed;
- (11) as 10, with `world.padding` held in the file (a) without `resolver_inputs`, (b) with `scheduled_by` `"README.md"`, which is neither `none` nor a path under `transcripts/sessions/`, the file's bytes still hashing to `world_types.sha256` → as 10 in each form;

Mutants, each with the twins that kill it: the exit `promote` left out of the procedure count (8: it reads 10 against 2, `met`); one added for the exit `promote` when the `promote` is already on the prefix (15: it reads 11 against 2, `unmet`); the bound read as strictly under five to one (7: `unmet`); a resolved forecast of a declared type, or its resolution, not counted as object (7: `unmet`); an unresolved forecast of a declared type counted as object (9 on G′: `met`) or as procedure (9 on G: `unmet`); a forecast of an undeclared type, or its resolution, counted as object (10 on G′) or as procedure (10 on G), or made to fail the criterion as the second draft had it (10 on G); the three fields not checked for the criterion (11a on G′: `met`); the shape of `scheduled_by` not checked (11b on G′: `met`); the hash not checked for the criterion (12: `met`); the resolution of a forecast from before the opening counted as object (13 on G′) or as procedure (13 on G); a forecast from before the opening counted with its resolution (14: `met`); a forecast of a type not beginning `world.`, or its resolution, counted as object or on neither side (8: the `counsel.undertaking` pair, and either way it reads `met`).

The pin does not test R8's second printed line beyond what the old twins test, and it does not test what a claim says, which the clause does not read. It claims the kills listed above and no others.

## What these two amendments leave with the sovereign

1. **Criterion 12 applies a bound the charter states, and nothing about it is asked here.** CHARTER §12: "Count procedure entries per object entry; at more than assize's 5 : 1 (20 : 4) in any 14-day window, stop and report." A3.4 gives the count, "object entries (forecasts and resolutions of world types) against procedure entries (every other entry) over the window", and the consequence: "a ratio above assize's 5 : 1 stops the layer and reports". A brief "may tighten the charter's bounds, never loosen them" (A2.4), and a `promote` "may be entered only once every pre-stated criterion of the window holds" (A5.1). So a brief may count less as object, which Amendment 2 does, and may not leave the bound untested. Row 69 records that "counsel drafts no criterion that a system with no skill passes". The bound is the charter's and not counsel's draft, and a system with no skill still passes it, as written under "What it does not show"; this text claims for the criterion only the count under "What it shows". To remove or replace the bound is to amend the charter, which row 33 records is done "only through a process judged as jack judges everything else" and row 69 places at C2c-3; and R8 already has it that the second printed line "is what the sovereign reads before deciding, at C2c-3, whether a scheduled stream is object".
2. **Criterion 10 is this brief's own, and counsel can neither keep it nor strike it on its own reading.** Neither the charter nor SPEC names an undertaking (the word is in neither file at `main` `3d13088a`); the criterion comes from R5. Three recorded rules meet on it. Row 70: "Counsel tightens the C2c-1 draft's criteria 10 and 12 in its delta before his read"; Amendment 1 is that tightening. Row 69: "counsel drafts no criterion that a system with no skill passes"; and one still passes criterion 10 as tightened, the row that costs nothing to keep, written out above. Row 54: "a change that relaxes a control is never landed on notice, only on his word in terms"; and to take criterion 10 out of the criteria relaxes a control on counsel's own conduct, which is a question counsel may not decide (row 35: "counsel may not decide a question in which it holds an interest"; the criterion's `args.proposer` is `counsel`, and by §5 counsel enters the window's promotion forecast). So by row 69 counsel does not put the criterion forward as a test of delivery, and by rows 54 and 35 counsel does not remove it. What his word would do:
   - *that it goes*: criterion 10 leaves the window file's `criteria`. R5's undertakings stay forecasts on the log, entered and resolved as R5 says. The status tool prints the line, with Amendment 1's count, and tests neither, so nothing in the window then tests that the rows of the inventory were re-entered or kept. A further draft of this delta makes those changes and is read cold.
   - *that it stays*: it is a criterion of C2c-1 as Amendment 1 leaves it, on his direction, claiming only what is written under "What it shows", with the system that passes it disclosed.

   Counsel urges neither. Until he has said, this delta is not finished, and "his one read of the delta" (row 68) is not asked.
3. **Row 68 still owes him a question.** It lists as owed "his word on lessons 1 and 2": "(1) A criterion needs a strawman as a pin needs a mutant: each criterion names the no-skill systems that must fail it, the court's code runs them, and a brief whose strawman passes is refused at its gate. (2) The drafter does not set his own bar: the reader names the weakest system that passes each criterion." Lesson 2 was followed for this draft as practice, not as law: the readers of the first and second drafts named the weakest systems that pass (runner #1012, #1018). Lesson 1 would be code, under a cap; no draft of counsel's builds it.
