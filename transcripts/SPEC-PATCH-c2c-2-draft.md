## 1. `wish`: the sovereign's directions, rulings and reads as entries

**SPEC §5, the `decision` row.** `kind`: one of `veto promote budget grant transmute fix wish`. The rule that follows applies to an entry whose `type` is `decision` and whose body's `kind` is `wish`, and to no other entry. For such an entry, `subject` matches `^[A-Za-z0-9][A-Za-z0-9._-]*(/[A-Za-z0-9][A-Za-z0-9._-]*)*@sha256:[0-9a-f]{64}$`, whole (SPEC's notation: every regular expression matches the whole value): a path of the repository and the SHA-256 of bytes. The rule is that match and nothing else: the kernel normalises nothing and looks for no file. Each segment begins with a letter or a digit, so the shape itself admits no `.` or `..` segment, no empty segment, no leading or trailing `/` and no path under `.github/`. A `decision` of any other kind keeps §5's present rule for `subject`. A body of any other type is not read by this rule, whatever members it carries. No required member is added to any type.

**Validity is the kernel's, as for any `decision`.** §8's steps apply unchanged, every one of them. That a `wish` is the sovereign's is not the kernel's question. It is the judge's ground 14, as §4 below leaves it after finding D5. The kernel reads `log.jsonl` only (§1): it tests no file, no tree, and no key state but the prefix's own.

**Replay.** Every log that is valid before this patch is valid after it, with the same state. There is one widening: a log that was refused before only because a `decision` had kind `wish` is valid after, when that subject has the shape and nothing else refuses it.

**SPEC §7.** No new state. A `wish` advances the head and the previous `ts` and has no other effect (§8 step 9's "the other decisions").

**What a wish is (SPEC §10, informative; the clerk).** A `wish` is the sovereign's signed entry naming a path and the SHA-256 of a text's bytes. It is counted by the acts evidence as a decision and not a repair, dated by the log, and named by hash so that nobody can later say he signed something else. It is an act, not an executable rule: the clerk evaluates no prose. One kind serves three uses:

- a **ruling**: the wish names the `transcripts/RULING-*.md` file that records his answer (answer 4);
- a **direction**: the wish names the file that quotes it;
- a **read**: the wish names the text a builder is to write code to (answer 5), wherever it lies: a brief, a patch such as this one, `RUNBOOK.md`.

The decidable part is presence. `tools/clerk.py` prints `W6 wish <seq>: <path>@sha256:<hex> — <commit> | absent`, where `<commit>` is the wish's *carrying commit* when that commit's tree has those bytes at the path, and `absent` otherwise. The carrying commit of a log entry is the commit, among the ancestors of the head being read and that head itself, whose `log.jsonl` holds the entry while the `log.jsonl` of none of its parents does; where that is not exactly one commit the entry has none, and the line prints `absent`. A wish *binds* a pair when its line shows a commit: he signed for bytes that `main` held when he signed. A later change to the file does not unbind the wish, since the bytes stay in that commit; and a wish entered for bytes that were not there binds nothing, then or later.

**A read list.** A wish may name a path under `transcripts/reads/`. Its list is the bytes that wish names, taken from the `<commit>` of its `W6` line; when that line prints `absent`, the wish lists nothing. The list's bytes are split at line feeds; one line feed at the very end ends the last line and adds none. Every line is `<path>@sha256:<hex>` in the shape above, and no line names a path under `transcripts/reads/`: a list does not name a list. A list with any other line — a blank one, or one holding a carriage return — lists nothing, and its `W6` line says `malformed list`. A pair that appears twice counts once. The clerk prints one further `W6` line for each pair, marked `via <seq>`, on the same test: the wish binds a listed pair when its carrying commit's tree has the pair's bytes at the pair's path. The kernel does not know the difference. This is how several texts are read in one entry.

**The verb** (`tools/jack decision wish`, after the push, by the builder's proposal). It refuses, before it signs, a subject of another shape, and one whose path is not a file in `HEAD`'s tree with exactly those bytes: he signs for what `main` holds. For a subject under `transcripts/reads/` it also refuses a list that is malformed or that has a line whose path is not a file in `HEAD`'s tree with those bytes. These are the verb's guards against a mistaken paste and are not validity: the kernel admits any subject of the shape.

**The prefix rule** (answer 5): "a read of bytes that extend bytes already read is a read of the extension". It is a rule about what he must read, and its test is mechanical. Where a wish names `P@h`, an earlier wish names `P@h0`, and the bytes hashing to `h0` are a proper byte prefix of the bytes hashing to `h` (both taken from their `W6` commits), the later wish's `W6` line is marked `extends <seq>`, with the latest earlier wish that does so; a pair named through a read list is marked the same way. The record then shows that his later read was of an extension. A wish can only name a whole file's bytes, so every check below asks for a wish naming the bytes on `main`; a wish naming a prefix of them does not satisfy it, because the extension is unread.

**The `W6` lines, exactly.** For each `wish` on the log, in the order of `seq`, the clerk prints the lines below and no others.

- *The wish's own line:* `W6 wish <seq>: <subject> — <where><marks>`. `<seq>` is the entry's `seq` in decimal. `<subject>` is the subject as signed, `<path>@sha256:<hex>`. The dash is U+2014 with one space on each side. `<where>` is the carrying commit's id as 40 lower-case hexadecimal digits when the wish binds the pair, and the word `absent` when it does not.
- *A list's lines:* when the subject's path is under `transcripts/reads/` and the wish binds it, the list is read from the carrying commit. If it is malformed, the wish's own line ends ` malformed list` and nothing more is printed for that wish. Otherwise the clerk prints, after the wish's own line, one line for each distinct pair of the list, in the order in which the pairs first appear in it: `W6 wish <seq>: <pair> — <where> via <seq><marks>`, where both `<seq>` are the wish's, `<pair>` is the list's line as written, and `<where>` is the wish's carrying commit's id when that commit's tree has the pair's bytes at the pair's path, and `absent` when it has not. A wish whose own line says `absent` has no list lines.
- *`<marks>`.* A wish's own line whose list is malformed ends ` malformed list` and carries no other mark. Any other line carries ` extends <seq>` when its `<where>` is a commit and some wish with a smaller `seq` binds, by its own line or by a list line, a pair with the same path whose bytes are a proper byte prefix of this line's bytes; `<seq>` is then the greatest such `seq`. Otherwise `<marks>` is empty. Pairs of one list do not extend one another, and a line that says `absent` neither extends nor is extended.

Written out, a line is `W6 wish <seq>: <pair> — <where>`, then ` via <seq>` if it is a list's line, then ` extends <seq>` if the prefix rule applies; or it is `W6 wish <seq>: <pair> — <commit> malformed list`.

## 2. `unread_text`: the read a text that claims force must carry

**SPEC §10, a fifteenth ground (the judge, not the kernel).**

15. **unread_text** — the proposal's head does not descend from trunk's head; or a path that the `read` list of `judge/scope.json` covers differs between trunk's head and the proposal's head, and some such path has no qualifying review on trunk.

**Covered.** A path is covered when a `read` entry matches it. An entry ending in `/` matches the directory. An entry ending in `*` matches every path that begins with the part before the `*` (the `read` list alone admits `*`; `paths`, `parties` and `counted` keep §10's rule). Any other entry matches exactly that file. The ground first requires that trunk's head be an ancestor of the proposal's head. A merge of such a head has nothing to combine: it leaves on `main` exactly the head's tree, whatever git's merge would otherwise do with two changes to one file, with a rename, or under a `.gitattributes`. The verbs already make proposals so (RUNBOOK: `jack proposal` refuses a branch that does not contain `origin/main`), and ground 3 already refuses a log that trunk's is not a prefix of; a hand-made proposal that keeps an older ancestry is what this refuses. The ground then looks at every covered path at which the head's tree differs from trunk's: added, modified or deleted. Call these the proposal's covered changes. A covered path that a branch changed and changed back is not among them, and nothing below reads it.

**A qualifying review for a path P** meets all of these. Each is decided from the history and objects the judge already holds — trunk's and the proposal's — and the log; nothing is fetched.

1. It is a file in trunk's tree at a path matching `^transcripts/REVIEW-[A-Za-z0-9._-]+\.md$`, and its bytes there were *brought* by a `proposal` entry whose signer's principal, under the law in force at that entry (§7), differs from this proposal's party (ground 7's party). One such entry is enough; an entry by this proposal's own party beside it changes nothing.
2. "Brought" is stated here whole, and is not taken from `tools/jack`. A `merge` entry m on trunk's log has a *carrying commit*: the commit reachable from trunk's head whose `log.jsonl` holds m while the `log.jsonl` of none of its parents does. Its *merge of record* is the commit reachable from trunk's head that has exactly two parents, the second of them the carrying commit. Where either is not exactly one commit, m brings nothing. The *proposal m merged* is the last `proposal` entry before m in the carrying commit's `log.jsonl` whose `commit` is reachable from the carrying commit and not from the merge of record's first parent; its signer's principal is the party ground 7 took for that proposal. A file with bytes b at a path in trunk's tree was brought by that entry when the carrying commit's tree holds b at the path and the tree of the merge of record's first parent does not. So the merge is what put those bytes on trunk, and the key that signed the proposal is the key that brought them. Bytes that reached trunk any other way — by a push, by a verb's own commit — were brought by no entry, and a review holding such bytes qualifies for nothing. The test is of bytes at a path and of a key that once brought them there: any merge on trunk's log that meets it counts, not only the latest to change the file. So a review edited on `main` qualifies for nothing while it holds bytes no such merge brought, and qualifies again, for what those bytes say and no more, when bytes a party brought are restored, by whatever hand. The judge's checkout holds trunk's whole history (`judge.yml` fetches it); where a commit or tree this test needs is missing, the review does not qualify.
3. Its first line is `VERDICT: ACCEPT` or `VERDICT: ACCEPT WITH OBLIGATIONS`.
4. It names P and the bytes read. A *read line* is a line of the file that begins `Read:`. Every read line is, whole, `Read: <path>@sha256:<hex>` or `Read: <path>@absent`: one space after the colon; `<hex>` exactly 64 characters from `0-9a-f`; and `<path>` everything between that space and the last `@` of the line, at least one byte. A review with a read line of any other form qualifies for no path. The review qualifies for P when one of its read lines has P as its path and, as its hash, the SHA-256 of P's bytes at the proposal's head; or, when the proposal deletes P, when one is `Read: P@absent`. A review may so name several paths. Lines are split at line feeds, so a covered path whose name holds a line feed can be named by no read line, and has no qualifying review.
5. Its `Reader:` line names one model, and the model's family differs from the family of every drafter's commit that changes P, as defined next.

A review names bytes and no commit. So a proposal made again by cherry-pick keeps its reviews, a review qualifies only for the paths it lists, and two paths may be read by two reviews.

**Drafters, readers and families.** A model id has the shape `^[a-z0-9][a-z0-9-]*/[A-Za-z0-9][A-Za-z0-9._:-]*$`, and its family is the part before the `/`. Every commit reachable from the head and not from trunk that is not a merge commit and changes one of the proposal's covered changes is a drafter's commit. A drafter's commit names its model in its message, and the judge reads the message's bytes itself, not through git's trailer parser, which matches a key in any letter case. Lines are split at line feeds. Take the message's last paragraph: with any empty lines at the very end set aside first, the lines after its last empty line, or all its lines when it has none. An empty line is a line of no bytes; a line holding a space or a carriage return is not empty. A *model line* is a line of that paragraph that begins, in any letter case, with `model`, then any spaces or tabs, then `:`. A drafter's commit has exactly one model line, and that line is `Model: <model id>`, whole: that capital, one space, and an id of the shape above. A drafter's commit with no model line, with two, or with one of any other form is refused on this ground. The reader's model is read from the review's bytes in the same way. A *reader line* is a line anywhere in the review that begins, in any letter case, with `reader`, then any spaces or tabs, then `:`. A qualifying review has exactly one, and it is `Reader: <party>; model <model id>; <date>`, whole, where `<party>` and `<date>` are each one or more bytes, none of them `;` or a carriage return, and the three parts are joined by `; `. That is the form the record's reviews already use (`Reader: reviewer; model google/gemini-4-argon; 2026-11-15T03:51:49Z`). A review with no reader line, with two, or with one of another form does not qualify. The reader of P must differ in family from every drafter's commit that changes P, including one whose change a later commit undid. That is strict on purpose: the ground cannot tell which drafter's words survive, so a path that two families have drafted on one branch is read by a third or drafted again.

**Merges.** A path absent from a tree has no bytes there, and two absences are equal; a deletion is a change like any other. A merge commit reachable from the head and not from trunk whose tree differs, at one of the proposal's covered changes, from the tree of every one of its parents is refused on this ground: covered text is not written, or deleted, in a merge. Nor is it brought back by one. For each covered change there must be a drafter's commit whose tree agrees with the head's at that path while its parent's tree does not: a drafter put there what the head holds. A covered change with no such commit — the bytes of an old commit of trunk merged in, say, before or after a drafter touched the path — is refused on this ground, since the reader would have no drafter of those bytes to differ from.

**The `read` list at C2c-2.** `transcripts/sessions/`, `transcripts/windows/`, `transcripts/runner/`, `tools/runner/`, `transcripts/reads/`, `transcripts/BRIEF-*`, `BRIEF-C1.md`, `transcripts/SPEC-PATCH-*`, `transcripts/AMEND-*`, `transcripts/PUSH-*`, `transcripts/RULING-*`, `transcripts/CASES.md`, `transcripts/COUNSEL-OPEN.md`, `LAW.md`, `RUNBOOK.md`, `tools/enforcement.json`. These are the texts that carry a rule or an acceptance criterion that code is written to (answer 5's scope b), the texts that claim force or index it (COUNSEL-OPEN row 34's list), and the notes whose commands the sovereign pastes. `COUNSEL-OPEN.md` is on the list because the clerk gives its rows force today: a row naming `W3 merge <seq>` discharges that writ (`tools/clerk.py`). The cost is a read for each batch of rows, which is counsel's and is the point. SPEC §10's block that mirrors `judge/scope.json` gains the same `read` member in the same push (the tests refuse a push that changes one without the other).

**Not on the list.** `REVIEW-*` (a review of a review is not asked), `BUILT-*` (§3), `REPLY-*`, `RESEARCH-*`, `QUESTION-*`, the reports and the evidence. They claim no force. Nothing here stops a builder writing code to one of them: that is A2.10's breach, and §3 says how far the push can see it.

**What the ground does not establish.** `Model:` and `Reader:` are claims a session makes about itself; the runner's issue is the only record of them outside the tree. The ground makes a drafter name itself, makes the review come from another principal than the proposal's, and makes the reader's named family differ from the drafter's. It does not prove which model read, or that it read. Who reads is still chosen by the drafter until readers are drawn (C2c-3, B4). It judges the tree a proposal would leave on `main`, which is the head's tree when the herald merges the head that was judged onto the trunk it was judged against (RUNBOOK, `jack merge`: "no other commit is ever merged"); a covered text added in one commit of a branch and deleted in a later one reaches `main`'s history and never `main`'s tree, and no check reads it there. A2.10's read by the sovereign is a different act from this one, and passing one proves nothing of the other.

**The same rule as the opening gate, moved.** `tools/jack push --opens` (#182) already requires, for a brief, a review on `main` brought by a proposal of another principal whose reviewed bytes equal the brief's. Ground 15 is that rule for every covered path and every proposal, in the judge, where a session cannot route round it. The order of acts: the drafter pushes the draft branch and opens no pull request; a reader of another family reads it and proposes the review with its `Read:` lines (its path is not covered, so no review is needed for a review); the herald merges the review; the drafter then merges `main` and proposes — last, so that the review is on trunk before the proposal is judged.

**Why the judge and not the kernel.** A read is a file in the tree and a line in it; the kernel reads bytes of `log.jsonl` only (§1). The ground reads trees and the log, which is the judge's domain (§10 preamble), and nothing outside git and the log (A2.3).

8. a deletion of a covered path with no `Read: P@absent` line → refused;
9. the proposal made again by cherry-pick, the same bytes at the head → admitted;
10. a reader of the drafter's family and another model → refused;
11. two drafter's commits of two families on one path, and a reader of one of them → refused;
12. a drafter's commit with two `Model:` lines → refused; with `model: <id>` in lower case → refused; a review whose reader line has four parts, or a second `reader:` line in any case → refused;

## 3. The push takes its facts from keys

A push is not judged by ground 15: it is the sovereign's act. So the verb checks what the judge does not. Every `jack push` changes the trusted base (the verb refuses any other branch: "nothing to push: propose and merge"), and every one is checked as follows. These checks are new in C2c-2.

**No trailer is read.** A2.3: "until keys exist for every party, the `Proposer:` trailer stands in for the signing key, and that substitution ends when the party's key is declared." A fact about a party at a push is therefore a file on `main` that the party's own key brought there by a `proposal` entry, as ground 7 makes a proposal's party "a fact, not a claim". The verb's present refusal of a commit with no filled `Proposer:` trailer stays as it is and proves nothing.

**Names.** M is `origin/main` and H the branch head, both resolved once when the verb starts. A *code path* is a path under a prefix of `counted`, or a trusted path (SPEC §10.1) other than `SPEC.md`. The *reader's own prefixes* are the prefixes that M's `judge/scope.json` gives, under `parties`, to the principal that brought the review and to no other principal (today `pins/` and `spec/vectors/`). "Brought" is §2's relation, whole.

The verb refuses unless all of these hold.

1. **The code read is on `main`.** `--review PATH` names a path matching `^transcripts/REVIEW-[A-Za-z0-9._-]+\.md$` that is a file in M's tree; `--review none` is refused. The file was brought by a `proposal` entry on M's log signed by a key whose principal, under the law in force at that entry (SPEC §7), has `pins/` in its `parties` list. Its first line is `VERDICT: ACCEPT` or `VERDICT: ACCEPT WITH OBLIGATIONS`. The verb no longer copies a review into `transcripts/`: today it writes the file given to `--review` under its basename, whatever that is, after its checks. The `push` entry's `review` member is the SHA-256 of the blob in M.
2. **The reviewed tree.** The review has exactly one line `Reviewed tree: <40 hex digits>`, naming a commit R1 that is an ancestor of H. So R1 is in the local repository, and nothing is fetched.
3. **The build is the builder's.** The review carries at most one line `Built: <path>@sha256:<hex>`. R1 and M have exactly one merge base, B1, or the push is refused. *With a `Built:` line:* it names a path matching `^transcripts/BUILT-[A-Za-z0-9._-]+\.md$` and the SHA-256 of that file's bytes in M. That file was brought by a `proposal` entry signed by a key whose principal has `tests/` in its `parties` list. Its first line is `Built tree: <40 hex digits>`, naming a commit R0 that is R1 or an ancestor of R1; R0 and M have exactly one merge base, B, or the push is refused. At every path outside the reader's own prefixes: where R0's bytes differ from B's, R1 holds R0's bytes, and B1's bytes there are B's or R0's (otherwise the build and `main` both changed the path, and the build is adopted and read again on a tree that has `main`'s change); where R0's bytes equal B's, R1 holds B1's. *Without one:* at every path outside the reader's own prefixes other than `SPEC.md`, R1 holds B1's bytes. A push with no build changes `SPEC.md`, for which its `Text:` line and wish account, and the reader's own files, and nothing else. So a change at a code path always has a `BUILT` file behind it, whatever `main` has done at that path meanwhile. `judge/scope.json` is a trusted path, so a change to it — the cap, a list, the `read` list — is a change at a code path, and every push with `--opens` therefore carries a `Built:` line. And outside its own prefixes the reviewed tree is the builder's change as the builder adopted it, on `main` as it stood when it was last merged in, and nothing else: the reader adds nothing there.
4. **The pins are the reader's.** Every line `Own: <path>@sha256:<hex>` or `Own: <path>@absent` of the review names a path under the reader's own prefixes and is true of R1: R1's bytes at that path have that hash, or R1 has no such file. Every path under those prefixes at which R1 differs from B1 has such a line. A build cannot change its own tests: the key that holds the pins has signed, by path and by bytes, for every pin and vector the reviewed tree changes, and by 5 nothing else under those prefixes reaches `main`. A pin committed after the reviewed tree is not in it, and is refused whatever the review says of its hash. The reader may change back a pin that `main` changed before B1: the pins are its own, and it signs for the bytes it leaves.
5. **The head is the reviewed tree and `main`, and nothing else.** At every path, under the reader's own prefixes or outside them: where R1's bytes equal B1's, H holds M's bytes; otherwise, where M's bytes equal B1's or equal R1's, H holds R1's bytes; otherwise the push is refused, because the reviewed tree and `main` have both changed the path since the read, and the read is taken again on a tree that has `main`'s change. So nothing the reader accepted is dropped from what is pushed, and nothing is added to it. `log.jsonl` is a path like any other: a branch adds no entry, and H holds M's log. A path absent from a tree has no bytes there, and two absences are equal.
6. **The texts.** The review carries at least one line `Text: <path>@sha256:<hex>`, each naming a file in M's tree and the SHA-256 of its bytes there; with `--opens LAYER`, one of them names `transcripts/BRIEF-<LAYER>-draft.md`. For each such pair, M's log holds a `wish` by the key ground 14 admits that names the pair, directly or through a read list, and binds it (§1). A change to `SPEC.md` is written to the patch file that orders it, and that file is what the line names.
7. **Descent.** For each pair of 6, two things hold of every commit reachable from H and not from M. Its tree has the pair's bytes at the pair's path: no commit of the build stood on another version of the text. And it has as an ancestor the carrying commit W of one wish that names and binds the pair, the same W for every such commit: the build began after his read. Where several wishes bind the pair, any one of which this is true will do. So a text that changed after his read and came back to the bytes he read needs no new wish, while a commit made when it said something else is refused by its own tree; and a wish that came before its text, or a list that came after its wish, binds nothing (§1). Neither test reads `main`'s first-parent history, which a push moves onto the pushed branch.
8. **No covered text.** H differs from M at no path that the `read` list covers, in M's `judge/scope.json` or in H's. And no commit reachable from H and not from M has a tree that differs, at a path either of those lists or its own covers, from the tree of every one of its parents: no covered text is added and taken away again inside the branch, where `main`'s history would keep bytes that no review named and `W6` could find them. Neither M's list nor H's covers a path the verb itself places with a push (the parts of the run of record, `transcripts/diff-`). A covered text reaches `main` by a proposal, under ground 15. A pattern leaves the `read` list only by a push, and that push's code review reads the change to `judge/scope.json` like any other; this check does not look back at what an earlier push uncovered.

**What is pushed is what was checked.** The branch's tests run inside the sovereign's repository and can do there whatever git allows: move a ref, install a hook, stage a file. So the verb does not rely on the state they leave.

- It fast-forwards `main` to the commit H it resolved at the start, by its hash, never to the branch's name.
- Every git command it runs after the branch's code has run is run with hooks turned off (`core.hooksPath` set on the command line to an empty directory).
- Immediately before it pushes, it reads from the object database the commit C it is about to push and requires three things: C has exactly one parent, and it is H; C's tree differs from H's at `log.jsonl` and at the files of the run of record the verb itself placed, and nowhere else; and each of those holds the bytes the verb wrote, by a hash it kept in memory. Otherwise it undoes everything and refuses.
- It pushes C by its hash to `main`, and nothing else.

*What this does not establish.* Code that the check runs is run as the sovereign, on his machine, with everything he can reach, the verb's own file included. The last comparison leaves such code nothing to gain from the repository's state; it does not make the verb safe from code that attacks the verb's process. The control for hostile code in a branch is the code read, which accepted every byte of H with its tests, and a check that runs where it can write nothing outside its own directory, which is not this window's.

**A new read or a new build is brought by its own party.** A file satisfies these checks for a party only by bytes that a merge of that party's proposal brought to its path (§2). After a fix the builder adopts the new tree in bytes its own proposal brings, and the reader reads it in bytes its own proposal brings. The templates have each use a new file, which keeps one build or one read to one path; the checks do not ask for that, and a file changed by its own party's proposal satisfies them for its new bytes. Bytes that only a push or another party's proposal put at a path are not that party's, and satisfy nothing for it.

**How a push is assembled.** The builder's branch is cut from a commit of `main` that carries the wishes, and ends at R0. The builder proposes its `BUILT` file from a second branch that holds that file alone: the code branch cannot be proposed, since it changes the trusted base (ground 1). The reader commits its pins and vectors on the code branch itself, which moves that branch from R0 to R1, and proposes its review from a branch that holds the review alone. When both files are on `main`, `main` is merged into the code branch at R1, which gives H, and the sovereign pushes that branch.

Every line of the review that begins `Built: `, `Own: ` or `Text: ` has its form, whole; a review with any other such line is refused.

**What these checks establish.** That the holder of the builder's key adopted the tree R0; that the holder of the pins' key accepted R1, which holds the builder's changes as adopted, `main` as it was last merged in, the reader's own pins and vectors, and nothing else, and signed for every pin and vector it changes; that H is R1 merged with `main`, path by path, with nothing dropped and nothing added; that the commit pushed has H as its only parent and holds H's tree with the verb's own entry and evidence and nothing else; that every commit `main` receives holds each text the reader named as the sovereign read it, and descends from a commit that carries his wish for it; and that no covered text came with it.

**What they do not establish.**

- *Who wrote a commit.* A party adopts a tree by its key; that a session of that party typed it rests on the runner's record. Sessions that cannot use one another's keys are the custody text's.
- *That the list of texts is complete.* The scope is answer 5's b: "every text that carries a rule or an acceptance criterion the code is written to". The `Text:` lines are the reader's signed statement of which those are, and the reader is asked for it in terms (the code-read template: name every text the code implements a rule of). A rule taken from a text the list omits passes every check here, and no check could stop it. BRIEF-C2c-2's R6, row a, names who is bound, what follows that breach (answer 2) and the rung the obligation stands on (answer 6): the fifth, attested by a party other than the one bound.
- *That the code was written after the read.* Descent shows what a commit was built on, not when it was typed. Old work committed again on top of W passes; where a breach has made a rebuild necessary, the independence of the rebuild is a question put to the code read, as BRIEF-C2b's question (8) was. And work typed after his read on a branch cut before W is refused: a builder begins from a commit that carries the wish.
- *Code that enters by proposal.* A builder's proposal under `tools/`, `tests/`, `sim/` or `harness/` is judged by grounds 1 to 15 and by none of these checks. For that code A2.10 rests on the session templates' refusal (#780), which is a prompt.
- *Families.* The push compares no model with another. That a code read is by another family than the build is the review rule of the layer's brief, kept by hand as today.

**The opening gate stands.** With `--opens LAYER` the verb's opening gate stands as C2c-1 leaves it, and this patch changes nothing in it. The rule is RUNBOOK's, under `jack push`, and is not restated here: `main` holds the layer's brief and its reviews, one review where the layer has no window file, and where it has one as many as that file's `numbers.readers` says, each read by the gate's own test for its verdict, its `Reviewed tree:` line, the bytes at that commit and who brought it. Checks 1 to 8 are added to that gate. Two things follow for the texts. A review of a brief or of a window file that is to open a layer carries a `Reviewed tree:` line, naming the commit of the drafter's branch at which the reader read them, beside the `Read:` lines ground 15 asks for; once the drafter's proposal has merged, that commit is on `main`. And the code review of check 1 may bear the layer's name: the gate counts the reviews that meet its test, and one that does not meet it is not counted and refuses nothing by itself. The gate asks two things of a brief's review that ground 15 does not, and they stand. It reads the commit that added the file, so the review of a brief that is to open a layer is a new file, and one changed in place afterwards is not counted. And its `Reviewed tree:` commit must be on `main`, so the drafter merges `main` into the branch that was read and proposes that; a brief committed again after its read, by cherry-pick or to add a trailer, is read again before it opens a layer, although ground 15 would admit it on the bytes. The gate also has residues of its own, unchanged here. It tests that the brief had its present bytes at the reviewed commit, not what the review says it read. And it finds the merge that brought a review by `main`'s first-parent history (`tools/jack`). A push fast-forwards `main` to a head whose first parent is the pushed branch, so after one the commits `main` held before it lie off that history, and the gate then makes its test at the push's head: a review that a non-counsel proposal brought still passes, and the gate can no longer tell that proposal from a later one whose branch merely held the file. Ground 15 and checks 1 to 8 use no first-parent history, only ancestry, trees and the carrying commits of entries.

**A moved `main`.** The verb today refuses when `main` has moved. C2c-1 is to make the verbs merge a moved `main` and retry, "every check repeated" (COUNSEL-OPEN row 68). Under that retry these checks are among the repeated ones, on the new M and the new H. Some then refuse rightly: a text of 6 whose bytes changed on `main` needs a new wish and a new read, and a path both sides changed is refused by 5.

**Before `wish` exists.** C2c-2's own opening push is made by the verb as it stands, and his read of this patch and of the other texts of §6 is stated in a `grant`, as at seqs 3432 and 4643. These checks bind from the first push after the proposal that carries them has merged. Between the two the old verb is the only one, and it checks none of this. Nothing prevents a push in that interval. BRIEF-C2c-2 forbids it by two criteria the window is judged on: no push inside the window (its criterion 3, as C2c-1's), and the pin `push_unread_code` killed at the exit (its criterion 4). So the first push after the opening is made by the new verb, or the window fails.
